Magmascope

How to listen to the magma beneath the Nether, find vents and break their crust in time with the pulse.

Magmascope looks not up but down — into a bowl of lava. Vents are found by ear.

…

Assembly

A three-by-three slab of heat-proof plates, the bowl-heart in the middle, two-block supports at the corners. It works only in the Nether and only with a full bowl: pour three buckets of lava by using a bucket on any part of the magmascope.

Hands-on

What to doHow
Start observingright-click the magmascope: your gaze drops into the bowl
Strike the magmaclick — a ring spreads out from the crosshair
Hear a ventwhen the ring reaches a vent it wakes an echo: the further the strike was, the wider and more shifted the echo
Clear the magmastrike almost point-blank — the vent’s crust becomes visible
Break the crustthe crust has its own pulse: rings converge on the rim. Strike the moment a ring reaches the rim
A missa strike off the beat heals one crack; a second strike on the same beat does not count, but is not a miss either

While the crosshair is on the crust you can hear the pulse — strike to the sound. Every vent beats its own way.

Embers of the deep

Fiery bubbles drift in the magma — catch them to get deep embers. The fire road needs them just as the other two need stardust and void shards. At the bottom of the vents lie the worlds of fire, reached by the bell.

The diving bell

A found vent of the Forge opens the diving bell. It is built in the Nether over a lava pool at least three by three: the skirt goes right onto the lava — it stands just above the surface — then the dome, crown and winch on it (the layout is in the journal). A cryo still makes the coolant: click the bell with a canister, or pipe it to a launch gantry by the winch. Every dive from the Nether burns an ember-tempered chain, and the winch hauls the bell home for free. It is hot below — wear a suit with a Cooling Unit.
