Modular armor

Sockets, the Module Bench, 28 modules, world harm and how to choose armor for a world.

The armor of the worlds has sockets for modules. Game armor has none — and game armor and Protection do not stop part of what the creatures of far worlds do to you. So the armor of a world you have reached, with modules of your choice, beats netherite there.

The Module Bench

Crafting table

Put a piece of armor of the worlds (or a multitool) into the Module Bench, then modules into its sockets. The bench needs no energy. A module can be taken out again and is never lost; an item taken away keeps its modules. Every module is crafted from matter of a world on a Module Blank.

  • Two equal modules do not add up, on different pieces either: the first one works.
  • A heavy module (the wing) takes two sockets.
  • A set's module gives the small bonus of its set in any armor of the worlds. While the whole set is worn it is silent: the full set already gives both the small bonus and the big one.

World harm

Creatures of far worlds strike partly with the element of their world: 15% of a blow on the second step of a road (Mercury and Jupiter, the Breath and the Hive, the Ash and the Mycelium), 30% in the late worlds and on the distant moons, 40–45% in open space, in the Nether and the End and on the far roads. The Moon, Venus, Mars, the Facet and the Forge have none. Armor of the game and Protection do not stop that part. What does:

  • every piece of armor of the worlds — its own shielding, up to 17% against the element of its set (each item page tells it);
  • Heat Screen, Frost Screen, Acid Screen, Radiation Screen and Void Screen — 35% off their element;
  • the spacesuit's own modules: coating and radiation screen 25% each, Cooling Units, Heating Units and the thermal unit 15%.

Hold G over a piece to see its sockets, what is fitted, what is silent and why, and its shielding.
