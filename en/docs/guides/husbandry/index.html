Husbandry

How to feed creatures of other worlds by hand, breed them and raise their young; herds sleeping together and dancing by a jukebox; shearing and gathering from the living, herds with a leader, predators and prey; taming and mounts.

Some peaceful creatures of other worlds eat from your hand, have young and keep their own routine: by day they graze and sniff around, at night the whole herd lies down to sleep. They take only the food of their own world — you will not breed a Martian roller on Earth wheat. The first three kinds: the Canyon Roller of Mars, the Moon Hopper and the Mist Lamb of the Breath.

Feeding by hand

A creature notices the food in your hand from a dozen blocks away: it sniffs, then trots after you and stops a couple of steps short. Click it with the food and it eats from your hand: a hurt one heals, a baby grows faster (each feeding takes a tenth off its time), and a grown one falls in love — hearts float up above it. If there is nothing the food would do (healthy and already in love), you keep it.

Young

Two of a kind in love find each other and, after a few seconds side by side, have a baby — with a wreath of hearts and a little experience for whoever fed them. The first young completes the journal stage “Young of Another World”. After having young the parents rest for five minutes. Bred creatures and their parents stay by the farm: they do not wander off or vanish while you are away.

  • Only in their own world. On Earth or any other strange world a creature eats but turns away: dim little stars fade above it — the wrong sky — and the line above the hotbar says it is in no mood for young here.
  • Not when crowded. With twelve or more of its kind around, food only heals: “Too crowded”.

Babies

A baby is half the size of a grown one, big-headed and paler; it keeps close to the nearest grown one of its kind and grows up in twenty minutes — faster if you feed it. When it grows up, it sparkles with gold.

As in the base game, a spawn egg used on a grown creature of the same kind gives a baby beside it (the egg is used up outside Creative mode). This works only for kinds that have babies; on the others the egg does nothing.

Rest, sleep and dancing

With nothing to do, a creature grazes by a plant of its world (it never eats the plant itself), sniffs, looks around. At night the herd lies down almost at once: the roller curls into a ball, the hopper folds its dish ears, the lamb tucks in its legs and the mist of its fleece settles. “z”s drift up from the sleepers. Dawn wakes them, and so does a blow, food in your hand or a player running past — walk. Flyers with folding wings (the ion vulture, the bolt petrel, the ember swift) land to rest on the ground, a ledge or a cloud top, and sleep only while perched; come close and they take off. By a playing jukebox and next to a working radio telescope, creatures dance: the roller bounces and rolls over as a ball, the hopper hops on the spot, the lamb sways in the air.

Shearing and gathering

Some creatures grow back what you take from them — no need to kill them. Click the creature with the right tool: what you take scatters next to it, the creature shakes itself, and a line above the hotbar tells you how long the regrowth takes. Until it grows back there is nothing to take; its regrowth food makes it grow back at once. Babies are not sheared, and a tamed creature only by its owner.

A sheared lamb is a smooth lilac barrel without its puffs of mist; the crab keeps stumps of the chipped spires on its hump; the grub's cap has no drips. Cloud Wool goes into Cloud Felt and the armor of the Breath. Shears, brushes and pickaxes of other mods work too. A chipped sparsaur keeps only stumps of its ridge, a brushed skink a stubby tail, and a glassneck's tear drops go dark.

Herds

Sclera Grazers of the Eye and Bone Gnawers of the Ash move as a herd: one leader wanders, the rest keep a ring around it and catch up when they fall behind. The leader is the one who came first; if it dies, another leads. A straggler finds the nearest herd within a couple of seconds.

Predators and prey

A hungry predator looks out for prey of its own world: the Ring Shark of Saturn hunts Ring Gliders, Bone Gnawers hunt Ember Mice. As it gives chase it raises the alarm — all the prey nearby scatters. Caught prey is eaten and leaves nothing behind, and a fed predator leaves everyone alone for a few minutes. Predators never touch your tamed or bred creatures, nor babies near you.

Taming

Three creatures of other worlds can be tamed: the sclera grazer of the Eye with a crystalline lens (Crystalline Lens), the storm ray of Jupiter with a float bladder (Float Bladder), and the under-ice whale of Europa with its own lumen (Whale Lumen). Feed it from your hand again and again: now and then it trusts you a little more — a heart floats up; a gray wisp means "not yet". Trust grows only toward the one who feeds: food from another hand, or a blow, starts it over. Once it trusts you fully, a golden ring spreads around it and it is yours (journal stage "A Friend on Another World").

  • Empty hand — "sit" or "follow". A tamed creature follows you, catches up when it falls behind, and appears beside you if you have gone far.
  • When you are away (logged out, on another world) it waits at home, where you were last together.
  • Release — sneak and click with an empty hand while holding its food in the other hand: it is wild again but stays.
  • Someone else's tamed creature can be fed (food heals it), but not commanded, saddled or sheared: "not yours, its owner is …". It can only be hurt if the server allows players to fight.

Mounts

Each of the three takes a harness made from its own world: Eye Harness for the grazer, Jupiter Harness for the ray, Europa Harness for the whale. Click your tamed creature with the harness and a saddle and straps appear on it; an empty hand gets you into the saddle; sneaking with an empty hand tells it to sit or follow; shears take the harness off.

A gait of its own under the saddle. The grazer trots, and when you sprint it breaks into a gallop — neck stretched, tail flying — and tucks its forelegs when it jumps; the ray beats its wings hard when climbing or dashing, glides in level flight and sweeps its wings back in a dive; the whale swims in long waves and drives with its tail on a dash. Mounts lean into turns, tip their nose along the way and turn their head where you look.

Stamina. Running (the sprint key) and climbing spend the mount's stamina — its bar appears above your hearts; walking and rest bring it back, food from your hand restores it at once. Out of stamina, no running. On the run, wind lines streak along the edges of the screen, dust flies from the grazer's feet, lightning from the ray's wings, and a glowing trail follows the whale.

A mount never leaves its world: with a rider it will not go through a portal, and gates, rifts and the mod's transport set the rider down. Wherever a mount already stands (say, placed with a spawn egg on Earth), you can ride it there. Two hundred blocks in the saddle complete the journal stage "In the Saddle". Log out in the saddle and the mount leaves with you and comes back when you return.
