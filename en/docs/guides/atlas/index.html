Atlas of Life

The encyclopedia of the creatures of other worlds in the Star Journal: who lives where and who eats whom, a species card with a living model, how to study a species, a hint for stardust and the world expert.

The Atlas of Life is a tab of the Star Journal that gathers the creatures of other worlds you have met: who lives in which biome, who hunts whom and who feeds on what, how to feed, tame and breed each one and what it gives. Every player has an Atlas of their own: on a server each one studies the creatures themselves.

How to open it

The Atlas tab appears in the journal header as soon as you have met any creature of another world. In a small game window the header may have no room for it; then the Atlas opens with the Open the Atlas of Life button on the pages of the creature stages: Beasts of the Worlds, Young of Another World, A Friend on Another World, In the Saddle, Life Between Worlds and others.

On the left are the worlds where you have met someone: each has a bar of met and studied species, and a world studied in full carries the expert's diamond. On the right is the world page or a species card.

Right-click a creature with the Star Journal to open its card straight away. A species you have not met yet counts as met right then, and its card reveals itself. The game's own creatures are not in the Atlas: for them the journal opens as usual. Esc on such a card takes you back to the game.

Search sits above the list of worlds: just start typing, or press / or Ctrl+F. The Atlas looks through the names of the species you have met, in the game's language (pinyin works too with Just Enough Characters), and lists them in place of the worlds; species not met yet are only counted. Enter opens the first one found, Esc clears the search.

The world page

  • Who lives where: a line for every biome of the world and the creatures that live in it. Species that live not in a biome but in ruins, come with events or fly under the open sky get their own line, Ruins, events and the open sky. Creatures you have not met yet show as a "?": the Atlas does not tell for free where they live.
  • Food chains: predators on top, their prey below, and the food of the world (its plants and fare) at the bottom. Threads run from the eater to its food, red for the hunt and green for eating, with sparks running along them. A thread shows once the eater is studied.
  • Masters and guardians: at the bottom of the page, the master of the world and the guardians of its lairs.

The species card

The card holds a living model of the creature: drag to turn it, use the wheel to zoom. The model plays its moves by itself (rest, move, strike, graze, sleep, dance) until you pick one; its looks are adult, young, shorn and harnessed, where the species has them. On the right are the study steps, the Next step (what to do next) and the sections that open step by step:

Sampleget a part of it by shears, brush or pickaxe where it allows, trade for one or take it by the huntwhat it drops and where its parts go
Tamed, Bredfor species that can be tamed and give young, as in the Husbandry guide—
  • A spyglass studies from afar and twice as fast: look at the creature through it, up to 64 blocks away.
  • Flocks of space are studied by the ship scanner: whatever it finds is studied at once.
  • A closed section shows as a dim name and the step that will open it.
  • Masters and guardians only have Met and Defeated: the Atlas opens their notes and loot after the victory. It does not tell how to beat them.

A species not met yet shows as an Unknown species. The Where to look button gives a hint for 2 stardust: the biomes, whether it lives on the ground, in the air or in the water, at what hour and whether in groups.

World expert

Study every creature that lives in a world on its own (masters, guardians and the species of ruins and events do not count), and you become that world's expert:

  • +8 stardust;
  • the creatures of that world are tamed sooner: every feeding adds trust one and a half times as often;
  • a pair fed by the expert is ready for young again twice as fast;
  • the Star Journal stage Atlas of Life (after Beasts of the Worlds).

New entries

When you meet a creature, study it or get a sample, a ring and a spiral of sparks rise over it, a chime sounds, and the species is marked New in the Atlas. The Atlas opens on the latest new entry; a new card unfolds as the mist draws down behind a glowing line. Jade under the crosshair tells whether a creature is studied: "Atlas of Life: new species", "met, not studied yet" or "studied".

The Atlas travels in a Journal Copy: a friend gets your meetings, studied creatures, hints, samples and world experts.
