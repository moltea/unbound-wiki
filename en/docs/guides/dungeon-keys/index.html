Dungeon keys

Where to find the floor key in every dungeon: what the gate socket tells you, how to spot loose stones and where the Gatekeeper’s Eye lies.

A world’s dungeon has several floors, and from each one a gate leads down. Some gates are held by a puzzle (mirrors, rune plates) or by the floor’s guardian, others by a keystone socket: it waits for a key, and until it gets one you cannot go further. The key never has to be crafted: it lies on the same floor, in its own room, and that room can be reached from the floor’s entrance without opening the gate.

The gate socket tells you where the key is

Press the socket with an empty hand (or with anything that is not the key). Two lines appear under the crosshair:

  • the first says which key the socket wants: “The socket awaits its key: …” (Jade and WTHIT show the same line when you look at the socket);
  • the second, in teal, says in which room of this floor the key lies and how it is hidden, for example: “The key is on this floor, in the gatekeeper’s room: behind loose stones under the eye carved in the wall…”.

The socket does not tell you the way to the room. The room’s name says what is in it (a store, a locker, a cell, a workshop, someone’s lodgings) — look for a room furnished like that on the floor; the floor’s notes on lecterns often hint at who keeps the key. Once you have the key, press the socket with it: the key goes into the socket, and the gate down opens.

Loose stones

Most often the key is walled up behind loose stones. You can tell them from four or five steps away if you know what to look for:

  • the mortar has crumbled out of every joint, the joints are dark, the stones sit askew, and a crack runs through them;
  • sparks flicker in the joints, and a draft blows from the gaps — dust drifts out of every open side;
  • now and then grit trickles quietly out of the gaps — you can hear it;
  • in Jade and WTHIT the stones are named like ordinary masonry, but under the name stands “The stones shift: they will give to a hand”.

How to open them: right-click them — with an empty hand or with something in it (just not while sneaking with an item). The stones crumble, and behind them is a niche with a chest. No pickaxe is needed, and it works in Adventure mode too. The Mining Fatigue that weighs on you while the dungeon’s master lives does not get in the way of loose stones.

Not every patch of loose stones hides a key: caches of supplies are walled up the same way (the room’s note speaks of them), and so are secret passages into neighboring rooms. The key is behind the one the socket told you about.

“A thing of the world” is a key that was not made for the dungeon but is found in the world (a core, a gland, a feather, a plate): the socket takes any such item, not only the one that lay in the key room. The dungeons’ own keys (the eye, the core, the seals, the hailstone and the rest) lie in the key rooms.

The Gatekeeper’s Eye

Gatekeeper's Eye is the key to the gate of the fourth floor of the Selenite Catacombs on the Moon, the Necropolis level. Without it the gate of the necropolis, behind which the Moon’s master sleeps, does not open. You do not craft the eye: it is walled up in the wall of the gatekeeper’s room on that same fourth floor.

How to find it:

  1. Go down to floor 4 and press the socket of the necropolis gate — the teal line names the gatekeeper’s room.
  2. Walk the floor: the gatekeeper’s room is a small room you can reach from the stairs from floor 3 without opening the gate. Inside there is a stone chair facing the wall, a bed in the corner, a table with a lectern and the sarcophagus the gatekeeper prepared for himself. A hunter guards the room.
  3. On the wall in front of the chair an eye is carved, its pupil glowing. Right under it are loose stones: crumbled joints, sparks, a draft. The gatekeeper’s note on the lectern says the same: the eye is walled in “beneath its own sign, behind stones that will give to a hand”.
  4. Press the stones under the eye with your hand — they crumble, and in the niche behind them is a chest with the eye.
  5. Go back to the socket and set the eye in: the gate to the master opens.

The room may have other loose stones too — a secret passage into the next room. The eye is under the sign of the eye.

The key is gone, or the dungeon is old

  • A dungeon from an older version of the mod. The socket’s hint works in dungeons placed before it too: the socket in the world remembers its group, and the text comes from the translation. Press the socket — the teal line will appear.
  • The chest is empty. The key room’s chest gives one key, and in multiplayer whoever opens it first takes it. Ask your companions: maybe they already have the key, or the gate is already open.
  • The key is lost. A world has more than one dungeon. The same world’s key from another dungeon fits this socket: the socket checks which item is set in, not which chest it came from. A key that is a thing of the world can simply be gathered again.
  • There is no room by that name on the floor. In dungeons of newer versions the key room always stands; if it is missing in a dungeon placed by an older version, take the key from another dungeon of the same world.
