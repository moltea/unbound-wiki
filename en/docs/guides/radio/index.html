Radio telescope

The dish, the radio map and the waterfall: how to tune the receiver to signals from the sky.

Stars are joined with lines, seams are traced with the crosshair, vents are heard in rhythm — and radio is tuned. The radio telescope shows no sky: at the top is a radio map, below it the waterfall: frequency left to right, time top to bottom.

Assembly

Radio Receiver sits on a mast of three Lattice Mast, and around the receiver, at its level, a 7 × 7 dish of Dish Panel. The receiver shows missing blocks as ghosts, and the last block placed assembles the dish by itself. The dish looks where it was aimed and keeps tracking that point across the sky.

A real sky

The map is the real Galaxy: the disc and the bulge, bright sources at their real coordinates. What never rises over Earth can be seen from the Moon or from your own station. Running machines closer than 24 blocks and thunderstorms cause interference — put the dish away from your base.

Hands-on

Every kind of signal has its own tuning control — the beam panel tells you which one to take, but not the solution.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.
  • Narrow band — lead the crosshair along a drifting line, and tune out the interference creeping toward it with the second button.
  • Dedispersion — the wheel shifts the waterfall until the arc of a burst straightens into a line; record it while the burst is visible.
  • Folding — the wheel changes the trial period: far from the right one there is noise, closer — slanted strokes, exactly right — a pillar.
  • Template — drag a comb of lines along the spectrum to the faint lines; they show once the dish has gathered enough signal.

Everyone around sees a find: the dish shoots a beam into the sky in the color of the source.

The repeating source

Almost every fast radio burst comes once. One source — in Vulpecula — repeats: once in five days it sends a series of three to six bursts in a night (the hint “when will the signal come” counts down to it). Point the dish at Vulpecula and straighten the arcs with the dispersion wheel.

Spoiler: this describes a place reached late in the game.

Each burst of the series caught with the dish on Vulpecula is a radio log of the magnetar; three are a navigation record for an interstellar drive of the Quasar stage (see Interstellar space). During the series Vulpecula may be below the horizon — then wait for the next series or listen from orbit, where the whole sky is seen.

The three-road interferometer

Three radio telescopes on three roads — in the Solar System, in the Interrift and in the Depths — listen as one eye once they are tied together by hydrogen clocks. Build a Hydrogen Maser from the blueprint (Interferometer Blueprint) and set it beside the receiver of an assembled telescope.

Every four days comes the night of Sagittarius (a day and a half long); outside it the maser console says when the next one begins. On the night press the maser and keep the crosshair on the drifting bright center for a minute — on every road it moves in its own way. You get a Night Record.

Spoiler: this lists rewards.

Three records of one night from the three roads are combined at the observatory console of your own station into a Shadow Image: an orange ring around the black shadow of Sagittarius A*, and inside it a thin, even ring. The image is the navigation record of the place where all three roads meet — see The Focus.
