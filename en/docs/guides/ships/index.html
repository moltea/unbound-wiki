Ships and space

The shipyard, ship forms and classes, modules, flight over a world and going out into space.

A ship is late-game transport. It flies over a world like a plane, climbs above the sky — and finds itself in the space of its road, where worlds are spheres in the sky.

…

The shipyard

A ship is welded at the shipyard from parts — which ones, the form page shows. The form sets the role (scout, freighter, warship…), the hull, hold, tank and the number of module slots. The ship’s class multiplies speed, hull, hold, jump and scanner.

You build the shipyard yourself: the shipyard console at the middle of a long side of a 9 × 5 slipway, two crane towers of four sections at opposite corners (the scheme and its Show in the world button are on the Shipyard page of the star journal); clicking the console shows what is missing. The shipyard knows the scout of your road once you beat the road's first master (Terminator Warden, Mother of the Comb, Giant's Remnant) and the hauler after the last one; other forms come from blueprints in space caches and stations.

Modules

Modules go into slots: thrust, hull, hold, scanner and others, each with tiers I–III. A tier is made from the metals of its road.

Flight

Over a world the ship flies like a plane: cruise, boost, turns, climb. Above the sky begins the space of the road: belts, comets, the peoples’ stations and anomalies. A ship can be picked up into the hold by a beam, towed, and you can transfer to another one.

From space a ship enters only the skies of worlds you have already been to: landed there by rocket, shuttle or bell, or came through the gates. Earth and the small worlds are open to every pilot. At any other world the hull stops at the edge of the sky, and the red line under the sight tells you why and what to do — for a planet, "Land here by rocket first".

Someone else’s ship (two players) can be refueled and mended by anyone, but only its owner takes the helm: a guest comes aboard through the stern hatch while the owner is home, and rides as a passenger in a two-seater once the owner is in the cockpit. Clicking such a ship says exactly that — whose it is and what it takes. Only the owner opens the ship console (modules and hold) — from outside and from the passenger seat alike.

While you are aboard — as the pilot or a passenger — you are behind the hull: claws, projectiles and beams of creatures hit the hull instead of you, at half strength. The hull does not last forever: a wrecked ship no longer shelters you. Heat, cold and radiation are not stopped this way — those are for the suit and modules.

Home: the landing beacon

From space a ship enters a world's sky where it touches the globe — come back to Earth from the wrong side and you can land twenty thousand blocks from your base. For free, the globe keeps the Takeoff pad — where the ship last left that world for space: fly within 1500 blocks of the mark and the ship enters the sky right above it.

The Landing Beacon is help that costs resources: put it under the open sky by your base in a world you enter from space (Earth, planets, moons, small worlds), and the ship enters the sky right above it from wherever it reaches the globe. Only you and your team (/team) see it: from the ground, a beam into the sky; from space, a glint on the globe and the line "Entering above beacon: name". By the globe the mouse wheel picks another beacon or none; the autopilot aimed at a world with a beacon stops right above it. Up to four lit beacons per player in a world; name it in its window (right-click; the window also tells whether it is lit and why not), on an anvil or with a name tag — the name shows on the system map. Rockets do not use it: they already land on your previous pad.

The beacon also works in the eight worlds beyond the End's rifts and at the bottom of Nether vents. A void or depth ship that flies into a world's hole comes out in the sky above the chosen beacon (the mouse wheel by the hole, as by a globe), and the Takeoff Pad mark remembers where it left through the hole. The Rift Shuttle and the Diving Bell land beside the beacon — a few steps from the beam, not on your previous pad: they have no wheel to pick, so they take the first beacon of whoever is aboard.

In the Nether itself the beacon needs open space above it instead of the sky. A depth ship coming up from the Depths through the vault surfaces in the Nether right beside your beacon, wherever it rose; without a beacon it surfaces where it dove into the lava, if it comes up within 1500 blocks of that spot (the Takeoff Pad mark is shown on the vault). It always surfaces in a free space big enough for the whole ship, never inside the rock; with no room anywhere near, it stays in the Depths. The Diving Bell returning to the Nether lands on the pad it took off from.

In the cockpit: labels, zoom, picking a target

  • Labels in the sky. Labels that would overlap fold into one, the most important, with “+N”: from the outskirts the Sun shows as “Sun +19”, not two dozen names. A label hidden behind a nearby body (a ship, a boulder, a comet nucleus, a station, a creature) fades. The F10 key cycles labels: all → target and important → hidden. Danger signs are always visible.
  • Zoom. Hold “pick block” (the mouse wheel) or your own “zoom” key in the controls: the field of view narrows like a spyglass, and labels spread apart and grow larger. The wheel sets the power (×3, ×6, ×12), the right button the filter (all, bodies, stations, anomalies), the left button picks a target. The card of the aimed target says what it is, how far, and whether you can jump there — and if not, why: the ship is on a tether, not enough fuel (how much is needed and how much is in the tank), closer than 1,500 blocks — fly there under your own power. The jump charges toward the picked target, and the autopilot leads to it. During a jump you cannot leave the ship, even one that took off from a landing pad: the sneak key does nothing until the ship is out of the jump.
  • Instruments do not overlap. Side panels (scanner, radio, anomaly steps, comet nucleus, combat) stand in columns; when space is tight the less important one folds into a line; key hints form a column in the lower left corner.
  • Ship friends in the console (Shift + right-click the ship) scroll with the wheel; type a name and the list filters.

The System Map (F8)

The F8 key (Ship: System Map in the controls) opens the System Map, a top-down hologram of the whole current cosmos: bodies, orbits, asteroid belts and fields, known stations and your team. It works in the cockpit, aboard and on foot in any cosmos; on a far road the same key opens that road's own map, and buttons lead to the Interstellar, Pattern and Section maps if you have them.

  • A fisheye scale: true to size near the middle and squeezed toward the edge, so Mercury and the Kuiper belt fit in one view. The wheel zooms toward the point under the cursor, drag to move. The rings are distances: from the Sun, from the ship (To the ship keeps the middle on your ship) or, on foot, from you (To me); Whole system shows the whole cosmos again.
  • Only your own finds: the map shows only what your ship's scanner has found: stations, expedition caches, rich rocks, fields, anomalies you noticed. Things that stay put are remembered after you leave the game; moving ones (the comet, creatures) read "Seen N min ago" and fade after five minutes. Close marks of one kind merge into a counted heap ("Expedition cache ×9"): click it and the map zooms in until the heap opens.
  • Distance and time: under the cursor, how many blocks it is from the ship (on foot, from you) and how long it takes at cruise and at your current speed; for the target the same, plus whether you can land there and whether you can jump.
  • Target: click a body or mark, Set as target, and the Autopilot to the target button (from the pilot seat) flies the ship there. The autopilot now also flies to fields, caches, rich rocks and anomalies, stopping forty blocks from their edge.

The scanner's Rich rock marks do not lie: every marked rock of a belt holds an ore heart of the ore the mark names, in its middle, right where the mark points.

Moving targets

Jumps to a comet and to the Visitor are led: the marker stands where the target will be at the end of the jump. At a comet you exit beyond the edge of the danger zone, at the Visitor a hundred blocks behind it, in its wake, matching its speed.

The Outcasts’ Ark is a jump target too: you exit beyond the range of its main battery.

Landing on the Visitor

The Visitor’s disc is made of large boulders. Bring the ship over a boulder from above and match speed, without pressing forward or up — the landing legs settle on the stone, and the ship rides along with the Visitor even with nobody aboard. Stepping out, you stand on the boulder and can walk on it; step off the edge or jump, and the Visitor slips away from under your feet. You cannot land on a comet: its nucleus tumbles.

The comet heart

After a scan, the comet heart shows as a marker through the ice. It is deep inside a large clump and cannot be taken by hand: dig with the mining beam (left Alt) through the crust and ice. The mining beam is a ship module: it is made at a crafting table from titanium, a lens, a coil and a battery (or traded from the Fear Drillers) and fitted into a slot of the ship’s console.

Anomalies, hands-on

The anomaly steps tell you what to do with your hands, and show an instrument at the moment of the attempt.

  • Slingshot — pass the hole closer than 260 blocks (but not closer than 40) on boost and turn: the arc must be 50° or more. In the sky there are rings for the 600 and 260 zones; the instrument counts the arc (“18° more”) and tells you if you are too far or too slow. Leave the 600 zone and the maneuver counts.
  • Ride the jet — during an outburst enter the ring at the base of the column (the “stream entry” marker), not the red one, and hold on in the sheath for five seconds. Where the nose points, there you are thrown — the instrument says toward what.

Crew rights

The Ship Friends page of the console gives everyone an access level. Click a row to make a friend, click its label to change the level; the rows on top are “Everyone” and your /team. The ship can be used while its owner is offline.

LevelWhat it allows
Friendlive cargo, towing the wreck into a hold, crossing over without asking
Passengeralso comes aboard and sits without you
Pilotalso flies, lands, jumps, autopilot, beacon and dock services; no console or hold
First Mateeverything you can — console, hold, hangar, upgrades — except scrapping the ship and changing rights

In the seat. The ship window has an inventory button: your own inventory with armor and the 2×2 grid, without leaving the seat. A passenger or a friend who may not open the console gets their inventory on E right away. The Call the Freighter button there (or its key) calls your freighter.

Passengers of another kind — animals and villagers — ride in the Bio Bay.

Dismantling

A ship can be dismantled or refitted only on the shipyard’s slipway: put it on the slipway and hold the “Dismantle” button in the shipyard screen for a second and a half.
