Facetkin

The Facet
The small glass folk of the Facet, whose heads are hollow cut crystals with a light burning inside. The light shows every mood, so a Facetkin cannot lie without going green. They call their world the Gem, gather its light, split it into colors and keep each color in its own vessel, hoping to pour the white light back together one day. Humans, who have no facets and no light to show, they call the rough ones.

Where they live

Facetkin Cluster. A cluster of the Facetkin: houses cut like gems and held in settings of four prongs, crowned with stepped facets, their windows lit from inside like the eyes in a Facetkin's head. They grow around a light well, as crystals grow around a vein, and above the well hangs a great prism that sends a rainbow across the paving toward the east.

What to talk about

Tell me about this world.

You are inside the Gem, rough one. Everything you see is one stone: the mountains are its facets, the golden veins are its blood, the glass spires are its bones. There is no sun here and no night. The light comes from inside the stone, the way it comes from inside our heads.

What are the golden veins in the rock?

The blood of the Gem. Light runs in them the way water runs in your world — slowly, deep down, and only on the steep faces does it come out to breathe. We open them carefully, with a small cut, and close them after: a vein that bleeds too long goes dark, and the mountain around it goes gray.

Why do the veins sometimes flare?

That is the surge. Every so often the light rises in all the veins at once, the mountains shine, and the prism moths swarm along the veins to drink. We stop work and drink too. If you are here when it comes, stand by a vein and watch — and count it: we count our lives in surges.

And the blue glass spires?

The bones of the Gem: prism glass grown straight up from the deep. Light that passes through a spire comes out split, blue on one side and pink on the other — that is why we build with it. Take glass from a fallen spire, never from a standing one: the golems guard the living bones, and they do not ask why you came.

What are the stars in your sky?

Glints on the far facets. The Gem is hollow inside, like my head, and its walls are so far away that their faces look like sparks. Beyond the walls is the Dark you came from. ...You are giving me a strange look. Are they not glints?

Who else lives in the Gem?

The mountains, sometimes: a facet golem is a piece of mountain that stood up to guard the spires. Leave it be and it leaves you be; swing a pick near it and it answers with a fist. The prism moths drink from the veins. And the refractors... those are lost reflections. They drink light, and they are never full.

Where do refractors come from?

From broken mirrors. When a mirror cracks, its reflection falls out and has nowhere to go back to, so it wanders the spires and drinks light from anything that shines. Strike one and it steps into the nearest glass and comes out behind you — reflections know every way through glass. Quiet six of them, and the ring on the plain will listen to you.

What is worth seeing here?

Everything our First Cut left behind, and everything the Gem grew by itself. Ask about one, and I will tell you what my light remembers.

What is the gate that leads nowhere?

The Prism Gate. Our First Cut raised it and paved the road to it, and the road stops two paces past the opening. They said it would open when the light was whole again — and the light is still in pieces, in our vessels and in our heads. So the gate waits. We sweep its dais every surge, so that it is clean when it opens.

What was your First Cut waiting for?

For this. Go to the eldest for the long answer; the short one is that they left us a gate and a broom, and said that one day we would need only the gate.

Who dug the round shaft with the stair?

That is a light well. Your wells go down to water; ours go down to light. The deeper you go, the thicker the veins in the brick, and the bottom is brighter than the sky. Our old ones went down to drink when they were too tired to open a vein. You can drink with your eyes, rough one: sit on the bottom step a while.

What is the long hall full of mirrors?

The Mirror Hall, where the Gem came to look at itself. Half the mirrors are broken now, and the roof has fallen over one aisle. The mirror keepers still go there, but nobody looks into the great mirror at the end for long: it looks back, and something in it is thirsty.

What is the great ring that hums?

The Resonance Ring. It hums when anything warm comes near, and the hum climbs with every step. Our tuners say it was tuned to the voices of the First Cut, which is why it never finds what it is listening for. Walk up to it slowly and listen: you will hear your own heart in it.

Who planted the crystal trees?

Nobody: the grove is the Gem's own garden. Cut a trunk and there are rings inside, one for every surge the tree has stood through — that is how we learned to count our lives. We do not cut them any more. We sit under the crowns, where the light comes down in seven colors.
