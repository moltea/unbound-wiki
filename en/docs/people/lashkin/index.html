Lashkin

The Eye
Fibers of the iris that fell like lashes, took root in the white of the Eye and one day stood up to listen. Where a face should be, a Lashkin has a closed lid with a little iris of its own beneath; it opens a crack only to speak, and it speaks in a whisper, because the Eye belongs to a sleeping god, and a loud sound runs along the fibers to wake him. They call humans blinkers: a creature that closes its eyes twenty times a breath and the world still stands is, to them, either terribly brave or terribly lucky.

Where they live

Lashkin Hush. A hush of the Lashkin on the white of the Eye: low houses shaped like closed eyes, each roofed with a pale lid of sclera stone and hooded at the back with woven fiber, with lashes along the edge and a doorway that is the slit of the lid — so that the Sleeper, if he ever looks down, sees only more closed eyes. The houses gather around a knot of vessels that rises out of the White, warm and beating, and the paths between them are laid with soft fiber, so that no step rings.

What to talk about

Tell me about the Eye.

It is the eye of the Sleeper. He lies somewhere beneath everything, so deep that nobody has found where, and he sleeps with his eye open. We live on it: on the White, by the green of the iris. In the middle is the Well — that is where he looks from. Speak softly here. Everything you say goes into the fibers.

Why did he stay and fall asleep?

He did not fall asleep, blinker... he lay down. That is different. The oldest whisper says: he lay down with his eye open, so that something would see him watching. One who sleeps with his eye open is not resting. He keeps watch the only way that lasts. And if he wakes... ...No. Not even to you. We cover the lid with two fingers and point up, and away. That is the rest of the sentence.

What is he like?

Warm. We see through the lid by warmth, and the deepest warmth in the Eye is under the Well. Not hot — steady. Like a hearth someone banked for a long night. The Wakeful wrote that the warmth is going down, very slowly, one breath of ours in a hundred. We do not write. But we feel it. ...It is going down.

The tablet from the Ring at the Edge says: the hunger watches the road.

...Shh. Shh. Then we are not his dream at all. He looks through us... past the White, past the ring, out along the roads... at that. Something watches the road, and he watches it back, with his eye open. Two who watch. ...Now I know why we whisper, blinker. It was never about waking him.

What does he dream of?

The Wakeful wrote down the dreams the Eye showed them: a blue world with a pale moon, a rusty one, a striped giant, a ring of ice... and far below, in the heat, a sea of gold under a red sky and ash that walks. We think they are only dreams. Some of us are not so sure any more.

Far beyond the stars I saw a great eye: a blue pupil, an orange rim, and a white star in the middle.

...Shh. Shh. You stood in it? Inside? ...The oldest whisper says the Sleeper has two eyes, blinker. One is the Eye we live on: it looks in, at his dreams. The other looks out — far out, past every ring — and keeps watch where dreams do not reach. Nobody had ever seen the second one. Was the white star warm? ...Then he still sleeps, and still watches. Do not go back to stare. What is stared at stares back.

What did the Wakeful not understand about the dream of gold?

The vessel keeper laid two stones from the dream of gold in a cold vessel's bed, and the vessel beat again. So his dreams are real places, and warm ones. A sleeper who dreams of warmth sleeps long, the keeper says. Nobody argued.

What is the black well in the middle?

The Well. The pupil. It is dark because he looks out of it, and whatever looks out cannot be looked into. The glances rise out of the Well. Strangers built a pier over it, and their flying boat is still tied to it, torn open. We do not go there. If you do — do not look down for long.

And the green ridges around it?

The iris. Fibers, thousands of them, running out from the Well like the rays of a slow sun. We come from there: a fiber falls the way a lash falls, takes root in the White and one day stands up and listens. The grooves between the fibers hold pigment, and the little mites drink the light there. Walk across the ridges, not along them: the grooves are deeper than they look.

Why do you live on the White?

It is quiet. The White is far from the Well, and his gaze passes over it seldom. And it is warm: the vessels run under it, and if you lie on the White with your lid shut, you can hear his heart — slow, so slow. As long as it is slow, he sleeps deep. Our vessel keepers listen to it all their lives.

What does his heart do in the Closing?

The vessel keepers say his heart beats slower in the Closing: he is sleeping deeper, not waking. A blinker put a hand on a vessel in the dark and felt it. The young still lie flat in the Closing, but now they giggle a little.

Who are you?

Lashkin. Fibers of the iris that fell and did not wither. We took root in the White, stood up and learned to listen; then we learned to whisper. Under my lid there is an iris of my own — a crumb of his eye. That is why we keep our lids shut: if I opened mine all the way and kept it open, he would see himself in it. And wake.

Why do you whisper?

Because a loud sound does not stay where you made it. It goes into the fibers, along the fibers to the Well, down the Well to the nerve, and the nerve carries it to him. A shout is a pebble thrown at his eye. We forgive you; you did not know. But now you know.

Why do you open your lid when you talk?

Because words come from the eye. A word said with a shut eye is a word you do not stand behind. When I open a crack, you see my iris, and my iris sees you: that is a promise that I am not lying. And when we greet someone, we open it all the way, for one breath. It is a small risk. You are worth it.
