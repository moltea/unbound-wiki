Terminator Folk

Mercury
Short, stubborn diggers of the terminator, the line between the scorching day and the freezing night of Mercury. Their ancestors walked with that line around the whole world; now they live dug in behind walls of salt, which drinks the heat of the day and gives it back all night. Every family keeps a mirror on a pole, and humans are "softbacks" to them: no shell, no claws, and hopeless at digging.

Where they live

Burrows of the Line. A settlement of the Terminator Folk: burrows dug into the plain, each roofed with a low dome of polished salt that shines like their own shells, and beside each one the family mirror on a pole. In the middle lies the Line Court, its floor split by a golden line into night and day, where the whole settlement turns its mirrors to the Sun at the long dawn.

What to talk about

Tell me about this world.

The world? The world is the Line, softback. On one side the Blaze, where lead runs like water; on the other the cold, where your breath would fall as snow, if you had breath. Between them runs the Line, the edge of the day, and it walks about as fast as you do. Everything that lives here lives near it.

What were they like?

Big and hot. That's all the track says, and a track doesn't lie. Where they stepped, the frost of the cold side melted and never came back — the only wet hollows on the whole Line are their footprints. Lead runs like water in the Blaze, and they walked through it without hurrying. We dig into the salt to sit out a day. They walked through days.

Is the Linewalker one of them?

No. Too small: he lands on every third print of their track, I've watched him do it. The dawn callers say he's their echo. When the First Walkers left, the Line stood empty, and the day doesn't like an empty Line. So something kept on walking in their stride. Something that remembers the stride — and not much else.

The tablet says: a warden was set by the road.

Set. Not born, not come — set. Like a stake at a burrow mouth so the hatchlings don't tumble in. Ha. Ten thousand dawns we sang to him, and he was somebody's stake. A good one, mind you: he kept the Line while we dug in. Whoever set him knew we'd get tired one day. Can't say that for the Blaze.

Why did you stop?

Because the Linewalker told us to. He led the Walk, the first one and the last. When we grew too many and too tired, he said: dig in, and I'll walk for all of you. We dug. He walks. Ask about what lies under the Line if you want the rest; it's not a cheerful story.

The Sun rose, stopped and went back down. Does that happen?

The Hesitation! It does. Sometimes the Blaze peeks over the edge, thinks better of it, sinks back, and only then comes up for real. The dawn callers say the Sun is shy of us; the masons say it forgot something. A hatchling born between the two dawns is lucky: it chooses its own craft.

How do you live through the heat and the cold?

We don't argue with either. Under salt and mirrors in the Blaze, by the salt that drank the day in the cold. And we eat the Line itself. Here, a bowl of twilight broth: gold on one side, dark on the other, salt in the middle. Eat it before you step into either.

Is there any water on Mercury?

Only as stone. At the poles there are craters whose bottoms have never seen the Blaze, not once since the world began, and there the cold keeps ice. Heat keepers make the trip now and then and bring blocks of it back wrapped in salt. Bring us ice of your own and you'll see a Terminator dance.

What is worth seeing around here?

Our holy places and our storerooms; for us that's the same thing. And the holes the softbacks left, but ask about those separately. That's another kind of story.

Who built the shrine with the golden ring?

The Walkers did, where the Blaze first burned one of them. The stelae look inward, away from the Sun: you never look at the Blaze, you look at what it lights. Stand in the ring with your back to it and you'll understand our prayers. They're mostly "not yet, not yet".

And the salt pillars in the hollows?

Salt orchards. The hollows breathe salt out of the rock, and where a seam of warm crust runs underneath, the salt climbs toward the cold like a plant. We cut a pillar once in a hundred dawns, and only from the top: cut the root and your grandchildren go without salt.

Who raised the obelisk whose shadow never moves?

We did, where the Walk ended. The shadow stays put as long as the Linewalker keeps walking: that's the bargain. The golden marks along it are the hours of the last Walk. If the shadow ever moves, the dawn callers say, it's time to walk again.

What is the crater of fused crust?

A piece of the sky fell there, hot enough to melt the rock. The drop of gold at the bottom is all that's left of it. Heat keepers go there for embers: the crust there has never quite cooled. Watch out for the walkers of cinder. They like it there too.

What is the salt dome in the hollows?

A bubble the hollows blew. Someone carved benches in it: maybe us, maybe the softbacks, maybe both. We go there to listen to the salt growing. It's very quiet, like a claw on glass far away. If you hear it, you've stood there long enough.
