Cloud Dwellers

Venus
The Cloud Dwellers once drifted in the cool cloud deck of Venus under living bubbles, until the great storms tore them open. They came down to the plains but never made peace with the ground, where the acid settles: they walk on stilt legs, live on piles and walkways, and rank each other by how high they live. Water is the dearest thing they know, and they greet a guest from Earth — the Dewdrop, as their songs call it — with wonder.

Where they live

Cloud Walkways. A village of the Cloud Dwellers: bubble houses wrapped in metal frost on piles high above the acid, joined by walkways and hanging bridges. Over the Mooring Court the last bubble of the Drift still floats on its tether, and the elder stands beneath it, for the most respected live highest.

What to talk about

Tell me about Venus.

Up there is the Deck: the cloud layer where the air is cool and soft, and where we were born, long ago. Down here is the Deep. Everything heavy sinks into it — heat, acid, stone, us. The sky is yellow because it is made of sulfur, and it rains all the time, only the rain never arrives.

Why does the rain never reach the ground?

It changes its mind halfway. The acid falls from the Deck, meets the heat of the Deep and turns back into cloud. We call such rain virga. On a virga day it is cooler on the walkways, and flakes of sulfur drift down like snow — the children catch them on their bells.

How do you bear this heat?

Our skin is wax, guest, and wax does not argue with heat: it shines it back. The acid slides off it too. Inside the bell it is cool — that is where we keep our thoughts. Your shell is clever, but I would not trust it on the Deep for long.

Why does the wind always blow to the west?

Because the sky is in a hurry and the world is not. The whole Deck runs around Venus faster than the stone turns beneath it. In the Drift we rode that wind around the world in a few days. Now it only pushes us along the walkways — hold the rail when it rises.

Who else lives on Venus?

The haze mantas are the sky's sieves: they swim through the sulfur and leave the air cleaner — never harm one. The acid toads squat on the tesserae and swallow the fog; leave them be, they spit far. And in the lava rivers live the serpents, who come out for warm bodies. We build high for many reasons.

What were they like?

Tall enough to stand in the Deep and breathe above the clouds — in the song their heads are in the cool air and their feet in the lava, and they mind neither. They tethered the first villages ring to ring with their own hands; the copper rings on our backs are copies of copies. Then, the song says, they went away to blow other skies. It does not say they came back.

Then why did the bubbles tear?

That is the verse we sing quietly. The Tearing came after they left. Nobody was breathing into the sky any more, and a bubble nobody breathes into goes thin and angry: the storms were the sky gasping for air. The young ones say the Sky-Blowers were called away to something worse than a storm. I do not know. I know only that they would not have left us for nothing.

The tablet says the road goes by the stars.

By the stars... and we have not seen a single star since the Drift. Perhaps that is why nobody came back for us: you cannot follow a road you cannot see. But you came by the stars, guest from the Dewdrop, and you came here. Then the road still reaches us, under all this cloud. I am going to sing that verse loudly tonight.

What is the glowing bell on your head?

My bubble. Once it carried our people across the sky; now it carries my thoughts, and a little light for the long nights. It still remembers the Drift: if one of us falls, it swells by itself, and we come down slowly, like a seed. Push me off a walkway and see — no, please do not.

Could I have a bubble of my own?

Of your own? You would have to be born with one, guest. But here — take these. Bladderwort bubbles, full of breath: bite one when your shell runs dry, and think of us.

Why do you live so high?

Acid is heavy, and heavy things settle. The lower you live, the more of it you breathe. So the most respected of us live highest: the elder speaks under the old bubble that floats above every roof, and children are born on the lowest walkways and climb all their lives. To us, "you have risen" means "you have grown wise".

What was the Drift?

The age when no one touched stone. Whole villages floated on bubbles, tethered ring to ring, and rode the west wind around the world. Then came the Tearing: storms that did not stop for a year. The bubbles burst one after another. The ones who held on to the rings came down together. The others... the wind remembers them.

Why is water so precious to you?

Because Venus has almost none. The sky is full of acid, and somewhere inside the acid there is water, the way there is a seed inside a fruit. Our condensers squeeze it out, one cup a day. A drop of it hangs at my throat, see? Every Cloud Dweller wears one. It is the first gift a child gets, and the last thing we let go of.

How do the condensers work?

A copper tower, a cold grate on top and a glass tank below. The acid fog touches the grate, gives up a little water and runs off as sulfur. The keepers turn the valves all day, chasing the cold. On a good day the tank fills a hand deep — and the whole village comes to look at it.
