Thalassia

The Sky Road
A second Earth under another cover: stone, soil, timber and ore are all here, but the grass is teal, the cliffs are columns of ochre basalt, and half the world is turquoise shallows with golden mats of algae. Pontus hangs over the horizon at a third of the sky; every noon it hides Hemera for a few minutes, and at night it lights the land. Three trees grow here: the parasol with its umbrella crown (four saplings in a square grow a giant), the tidewood on stilts in the mud, and the lanternwood whose berries glow. The tidestone below hides the ores of home and aquamarine in the cliffs. No suit is needed: live here as long as you like.

People

  • Thalassians — Amphibious farmers of the tide fields under Pontus, with a crown of amber gills. They sow sea barley where the tide has gone out and reap it before the water comes back; at noon, when Pontus closes the Warm Eye, the fields rest, and so do they. They count time in tides and shadows, read the night glow of Pontus like weather, and call a guest a gardener without a field until the guest has grown something.
  • Tidefield Hamlet. A hamlet of the Thalassians on the meadows above the shallows: timber houses of parasol on tidestone plinths, roofs of tidewood and lanternwood, barley beds between the lanes. In the middle stands the Eclipse Mast with blue Pontus in its rings, and at noon, when the giant closes the sun, the whole hamlet rests in its shadow.

Structures

Drowned Hamlet

A village on piles in the shallows: walkways to a stone square with a bell, a net house, a hall for drying golden mats, a boat shed and the elder's house. The water did not come in a storm. It came a hand a year, and the gauge by the elder's house is marked to the deck. They raised the floors twice; the elder's old stone story still stands under the new one, full of sea. Then they took the boats and went up to the meadows, and whoever lives there now plows.

Tide Lighthouse

A banded tower of cream tuff on the basalt columns of the coast, with a lens at the top that still turns and throws its light over the shallows at night. The keeper lit it at every high water, so that the boats could come in over the bars. The gauge on the seaward side is marked in his hand to the seventh notch. His last entry says the boats have gone up the coast and he will keep the light until he cannot see them.

Weather

  • Aurora of Pontus. Thalassia orbits inside the magnetic field of Pontus: at night curtains of teal and violet light hang over the whole sky, not only over the poles.
  • Tide Mist. Before dawn the warm shallow sea steams: the shallows and lowlands drown in turquoise mist.
