Addons

The Java API for addon makers: add your own journal nodes, worlds, constellations, events and gate conditions with a method call, on Fabric and NeoForge.

Unbound has a public Java API for addons. With it an addon adds its own content to the mod's systems with a method call — a journal node, a world for rockets, a constellation, a world event, a gate condition, a climate zone, a whole people of natives with your own body or a task for one, a creature with the mod's tricks and keeping, a boss with the mod's boss bar, a weapon with mastery, a console for a machine, a spacesuit module, a substance for the spectral laboratory, a radio source, a rift or vent leading to your world, a body in open space, a starship hull with your own model, a ship module, a station order, a HUD element or a strong moment — reads their state and listens to their events. Some things need no API at all: they are open through the game's data (see “Open through data”), and nine catalogs of the mod take entries from your JSON files (see “The mod's catalogs from your files”). No mixins, no access wideners, no copying of the mod's logic.

  • The contract is the package me.moltea.unbound.api and its subpackages. Everything else in the mod is internal and may change in any release.
  • The API has its own version — UnboundApi.VERSION (now 1.5.0, semantic versioning): a changed or removed method bumps the major number, a new one bumps the minor number; removals are marked @Deprecated at least one minor version ahead.
  • The same API on Minecraft 26.3, 1.21.1 and 1.20.1, on Fabric and NeoForge: method signatures do not change between game versions, only the game's own types do (for example, Identifier on 26.3 is ResourceLocation on 1.21.1). Build your addon against the mod's jar for your game version.
  • Javadoc of the package is in English; the overview is in api/package-info.java.
  • Your own blocks, items and creatures you register the usual way, in your namespace; the API connects them to the mod's systems by id or by supplier.

Getting started

Implement UnboundAddon. The mod calls register once per game start, on the client and on the dedicated server, before its own lists are read — the load order of mods does not matter. Install your addon on the server and on every player, like the mod itself.

  • Fabric — list the class in your fabric.mod.json under the entrypoint key equal to the mod id (the same word as the mod's command; in code — UnboundApi.modId()):
  • NeoForge — nothing to declare: a class of your mod that directly implements UnboundAddon and has a public constructor without arguments is found on its own.

Rules shared by all areas:

  • Registrations are static methods with the AddonContext as the first argument. They work only inside register and with its own context; otherwise they throw IllegalStateException.
  • Ids. Things are named by Identifier: the mod's own — <mod id>:mars, <mod id>:solar_eclipse; yours — ctx.id("path"). Progress keys are strings; a journal node's id is also its progress key.
  • Texts of your content are translation keys in your namespace (listed in each section below). Without a translation the mod shows the path in words, not a raw key.
  • Events are fields of the <Area>Events class of each area. They fire on the server, once per happening; an event nobody listens to costs nothing.
  • Energy, fluids and items of machines go through the loaders' own standards (Team Reborn Energy and Fabric Transfer on Fabric, capabilities on NeoForge) — see “Compatibility”. There is no separate API for them.

Journal

Package api.journal. Your own chapter and nodes of the star journal; goals; checking and granting progress keys.

  • A chapter becomes a tab after the mod's own; a node's place on the canvas is laid out by its links, like the mod's nodes.
  • Goals: granted() (your code grants it), item, items, key, either, alone, custom (checked once a second while the node is open).
  • Queries on the server: Journal.has, Journal.grant, Journal.state; on the client — ClientJournal.has.
  • Events: KEY_GRANTED, NODE_COMPLETED.
  • Texts: guide.<namespace>.chapter.<path>, guide.<namespace>.node.<path>.title|task|text.

Travel

Package api.travel. A destination for your dimension: rockets, the End shuttle or the Nether bell fly there, land and return like on the mod's worlds; on the space road it has a ball on the star map and, if you wish, in the Solar System for starships.

  • The dimension itself (generator, dimension type with gravity, air and heat) is your own data.
  • A destination with a discovery key stays hidden until the player has the key; grant it from your code, a journal node or the sky.
  • Queries: Travel.destinations, Travel.destination(id), Travel.destinationOf(dimension).
  • Events: LAUNCH, LANDING (with a first-landing flag), SHIP_TRAVEL (a starship leaves or enters a world, jumps, takes a far road or an interstellar jump).
  • Texts and pictures: name destination.<namespace>.<path> and ….facts; surface map textures/space/<path>.png; shuttle and bell window sprite passage/<path>.

Sky

Package api.sky. Your own constellation, body (moon, planet) or deep-sky sight (nebula, cluster, galaxy) in the telescope sky.

  • A body or a sight may wait for keys (requires); a completed constellation grants your keys (grants).
  • Queries: hasCompleted, hasDiscovered, progress keys, lists of all constellations and bodies.
  • Events: CONSTELLATION_COMPLETED, BODY_DISCOVERED.
  • Texts: constellation.<namespace>.<path>, sky_body.<namespace>.<path>, sky_sight.<namespace>.<path>.

Rifts and vents

Package api.sky. Your own rift in the sky of the End and vent in the magma of the Nether. Players find them with the mod's riftscope and magmascope exactly like the mod's eight — follow the void sparks and tear the seam, strike the magma and crack the crust in time with its pulse — and the world behind them is your destination of the shuttle or bell road.

  • Rifts hang 20–82° above the horizon, vents lie 15–82° below it; keep clear of the mod's (azimuths 55, 165, 250, 330 and 40, 130, 225, 310) — a place too close is refused at register().
  • leadsTo takes a destination of the same road (Craft.RIFT_SHUTTLE for a rift, Craft.DIVING_BELL for a vent); finding the place grants the destination's discovery key. fromStage(n) waits for the End or Nether stage; requires and grants work like in the sky.
  • Queries: riftKey, ventKey, hasFoundRift, hasFoundVent, rifts(), vents(), beyondRift, belowVent.
  • Texts: sky_rift.<namespace>.<path> (.facts, .clue), sky_vent.<namespace>.<path>; pictures — GUI sprites observatory/rift/<path>, observatory/vent/<path> and <path>_crust in your namespace.
  • Your places do not change the stages of the End and the Nether — count them yourself with Stages.advance if you want to.

World events and weather

Package api.worldevent. A rare world event or a weather in any world — the mod's, yours or a vanilla one — built from ready-made effects (sky and fog color, daylight, particles, wind, hail, falling items, swarms, sounds) and your own code.

  • Environment effects take the attribute by its id, the same on every Minecraft version the mod supports: Effects.scale(Identifier.withDefaultNamespace("visual/fog_end_distance"), Effects.Op.MULTIPLY, 0.4F), tint, recolor, flag (API 1.1.0). The older forms with the game's EnvironmentAttribute type exist only on Minecraft 26.x and are deprecated.
  • Your own effect runs once every every ticks and only while players are there.
  • The mod's commands start and stop your event by id: <command> event <namespace>:<path>.
  • Queries: running, isRunning, strength, start, stop.
  • Events: EVENT_STARTED, EVENT_ENDED, WEATHER_STARTED, WEATHER_ENDED.
  • Meteorites (API 1.5.0). Meteors.kinds(), Meteors.drop(level, player, kind, size, natural) and Meteors.dropAt(...) drop a meteorite of a kind and size; event: MeteorEvents.LANDED.
  • Texts: event.<namespace>.<path>.name|hint, weather.<namespace>.<path>.

Nether and End stages

Package api.awaken. The Nether and the End change for the whole server as players find rifts and vents (stages 0–4, see The Nether and the End). Read the stage, react to its change, add events and weathers that come only from a stage on, and count your own content toward it.

  • Queries: Stages.stage(level) and reached(level, n) on both sides; on the server also stage(server, Level.END), enabled, opened (found rifts or vents as ids), places, name. Client only: ClientStages.
  • fromStage(n) (1–4) only for events and weathers of the Nether or the End; below the stage the schedule waits. The mod's commands still start them at any stage.
  • STAGE_CHANGED fires once per change of the shown stage: growth, the stage command, the owner's switch.
  • Stages.advance(level, n, player) raises the world to at least stage n, with the mod's notice and moment; it only grows.
  • If the server owner turned the changes off, every query answers stage 0 and stage-bound events and weathers do not come.

Gates and creatures

Packages api.gate and api.creature. Your own condition for waking the gates of any world; a gates rule for your own world; boss events and creature tags.

  • Conditions add up: the gates wake when all of them are met. Your condition is a line in the gates window with a check mark.
  • Queries: Gates.conditions, Gates.worthy, Bosses.isBoss, Bosses.rank.
  • Events: GateEvents.AWAKENED, GateEvents.TRAVELED, CreatureEvents.BOSS_SUMMONED, CreatureEvents.BOSS_DEFEATED.
  • Tags: acid_immune — acid does not burn the creature; c:bosses — the common tag for bosses.

Addon creatures

Package api.creature. Your own creature — any Mob class of your addon: a Sheep or Pig subclass with the game's renderer, a GeckoLib creature — gets the mod's tricks, keeping and a page in the Atlas of Life without extending any class of the mod. Register the entity type yourself; describe the species by the same id with Species.add, then connect three hooks in your class.

  • Tricks: nine kinds (BREATH, COIL, LOB, RAM, DIVE, LUNGE, HOWL, POUNCE, SLAM) with a fair warning — a glow at the mouth, a mark on the ground — then the blow and a rest; they start only at a target your creature already has.
  • Keeping: taming by trust (sit and follow on the owner's empty hand, let go by sneaking with the food in the other hand), shearing with regrowth (and regrowth food), healing food, breeding (an Animal) only under its own sky and not when crowded. The mod saves the state with the creature and tells the client "sheared, tamed, sitting".
  • Atlas of Life: met, studied (watched at work for half a minute), sample (its part in the inventory), tamed, bred — each player's own; a right click with the Star Journal opens its card. Texts: entity.<ns>.<path>, creature.<ns>.<path>.lore.
  • Queries and events: Species.sheared/tamed/sitting/owner, hasMet/hasStudied/hasSample/hasTamed/hasBred, HusbandryEvents.TAMED, SHEARED, BRED, STUDIED — for the mod's creatures too.
  • Not open: mounts, herds, sleep and hunting (behavior of the mod's own creature class).

Bosses and dungeons

Packages api.creature and api.dungeon. Make your creature — your own class or a game one — a boss of the mod: the ornamental bar with phases, hints and attack warnings, invulnerable phase transitions, the victory key, the summon at the mod's dungeon altar. Your boss keeps its own AI and attacks; beams and particles of the mod's bosses are yours to use.

  • Phases: the first at 1.0, the next at lower shares of health; each has a color, a hint under the bar and, if you like, a strong moment. Entering a phase, the boss is invulnerable for transition(ticks) (30) and flashes in its color.
  • Bar: BossBar.borrow("<mod boss>") takes the art of one of the mod's bosses; BossBar.own(id, phases, compact) — your sprites <path>_frame_<n>, _fill_<n>, _end_left_<n>, _end_right_<n>, _crest_<n> in your GUI atlas. guard() — a lair guardian (compact bar, key elite/<addon>:<path>).
  • Attack warnings: attack(name, text) in the description, Bosses.warn(boss, name) at the start of the wind-up.
  • Arrows: like the mod's masters and guardians, your boss takes only part of projectile damage and, after a streak of hits, turns projectiles back for three seconds (a ring of shield, a blow up close breaks it). By default — as a mod boss of the same rank and flight (master 0.6 and 6 hits, guardian 0.5 and 5, flying 0.75 and 8 / 0.6 and 6); your own numbers — projectiles(share, volley), projectiles(1.0, 0) turns it off.
  • Victory: every player who hit the boss or was its target gets Bosses.victoryKey(id) = boss/<addon>:<path>.
  • Altar: put the Master's Altar <mod id>:boss_altar in your structure with Dungeons.altarData(boss, offering, count, group) and Sealed Stones <mod id>:sealed_stone with {group:"…"} (or Dungeons.placeAltar / placeSeal from code). A player with the offering calls your boss; the arena mechanisms of the group wake, and when the boss falls the seals crumble. Dungeons.openSeals opens them from your own puzzle.
  • Look of the mod's bosses: Bosses.beam(...) (kinds plain, prism, gaze, sun, frost, bolt, column_*…), Bosses.particles(...) / Bosses.particle(...) (spark, mote, ring, rune, ember, frost…).
  • Events: CreatureEvents.BOSS_SUMMONED, BOSS_PHASE, BOSS_DEFEATED; DungeonEvents.PUZZLE_SOLVED, ALTAR_AWAKENED, ALTAR_CLEARED. Queries: Bosses.isBoss, rank, id, phase, victoryKey.
  • Not open: the mod's attacks and walking of its bosses, your own beam kinds, the gate heart of a master.

Gear

Package api.gear: your own weapon abilities and perks, weapons and artifacts with the mod's logic, armor sets of your items.

  • Gear.addAbility(ctx, path, ability) and Gear.addPerk(ctx, path, perk) add kinds by id; a weapon or an artifact may also use the mod's kinds (<mod id>:wave, <mod id>:effect) with its own numbers.
  • Gear.weapon(spec, properties) and Gear.artifact(spec, properties) return a ready Item of the mod's class — register it yourself (Fabric: in register; NeoForge: your DeferredRegister, because register runs after the registries). It gets the ability on its trigger with a cooldown, mastery levels that change the numbers, the gear tooltip, "Hold [G]" and the details screen.
  • The model is a usual item model of your namespace. Texts: gear.<ns>.<path>.role, .ability, .ability.desc, .level.<n>, .effect (artifact), gear.<ns>.world.<origin>, item.<ns>.<path>.lore.
  • An artifact goes into Trinkets and Curios slots through one tag: <mod id>:artifact_slot/necklace (ring, belt, back).
  • Gear.addArmorSet(ctx, path, spec) gives your armor items set tiers (perks and bonuses for 2–4 pieces).
  • Modular armor (API 1.5.0). Gear.module(ModuleSpec, properties) makes an armor module (places, weight, group, perks, bonuses, home worlds) — register the item yourself; the Module Bench fits it by the mod's rules. Gear.addSocketed(ctx, predicate, SocketedItem) gives sockets (up to four) to your own items. Queries: sockets, fittedModules, wornModules, fitModule, worldHarmShield; event: GearEvents.MODULE_FITTED. World harm is open as damage types <mod id>:world_heat|cold|acid|radiation|void and the tag <mod id>:world_harm.
  • Queries: masteryLevel, abilityReady, wornPerks; events: GearEvents.ABILITY_CAST, GearEvents.MASTERY_LEVEL.
  • Not open: a GeckoLib model of the mod for your weapon, the mod's own armor class.

Natives

Package api.natives. Your own peoples of natives, your own tasks for any people (parcels, personal goods), topics of talk and trade offers; what a player has with a people; task and trade events. Package api.ringwright: let your puzzle or fight be the trial of a Ringwright niche.

  • A people is the id of its creature ("lunarian", Natives.peoples()), a job is a string ("elder"). A task is an Identifier: <addon>:<people>/<path>.
  • Your task works like ours: the "!" and "?" over the native's head, the task card in the talk, the Tasks window (F7), the corner tracker, the trust of the people. Goals: bring, have, slay, key, counted (your code calls Natives.advance), check (your own test). Rewards: item, xp, learn, discount, custom. Texts: quest.<addon>.<people>.<path>.title, .brief, .offer, .waiting, .ready, .done.
  • A topic's question goes into the people's "What's new?" (or under(...) another topic); its answer has nested topics, "back" and "farewell". Texts: topic.<addon>.<path>.ask, .text.
  • A trade offer joins the native's offers when the native reaches that level of its job.
  • Queries: taken, isTaken, isReady, isCompleted, doneKey, learned, met, trust, trustLevel, people, job.
  • Events: NativeEvents.QUEST_TAKEN, QUEST_COMPLETED, QUEST_ABANDONED, TRADED.
  • Ringwrights.pass(player, tablet) grants trial/<tablet>: a niche with the trial condition then gives its tablet.

Peoples of your own

Since API 1.4.0 an addon adds a whole people of natives with its own body: your GeckoLib model, texture and animations, your jobs, a chief and named masters, lines of talk and trades. Its natives live like the mod's peoples — the mod's talk window with a live portrait, the trade window in the people's color, the "!" and "?" marks, tasks and trust, the Jade tooltip and the "Trades" page of JEI and REI. Tasks may now send a parcel to another people (yours or the mod's) and reward personal goods.

  • The body: the model <addon>:native/<path> and the texture <addon>:textures/native/<path>.png by default. On 26.3 the files are geckolib/models/native/… and geckolib/animations/native/…, on 1.21.1 and 1.20.1 — geo/native/… and animations/native/…; ship both to support all three. The model stands at the origin facing north, with a head bone and the animations idle, move, talk, work, greet, trade.
  • The talk: without your own nodes the people gets the mod's plain talk — greeting, news (your topics go there), the job's node with trading, farewell. Texts are keys native.<addon>.<path>.greeting, .again, .news, .news.ask, .farewell, .craft_<job>, .people, .job.<job>; add lines with conditions (NativeCondition.persona, job, firstMeeting) or whole nodes (dialog).
  • In the world: Natives.spawn(level, pos, people, job, talkative), a command or your own structure — the entity is <mod id>:addon_native with the data people, job, talkative. A settlement of your people is your own structure; the mod's villages stay the mod's.
  • A parcel: NativeQuestGoal.deliver(item, count, people, job) with .given() (the giver hands it over) and .replies(...) — what the recipient says. Personal goods: NativeQuestReward.goods(job, offer) — one more trade for this player only while trading.
  • The same in your natives.json: a new people (an entry in your namespace with jobs and body), and trades and topics for any people.

Air, climate and gravity

Package api.life. Is there air, how hot it is, what gravity an entity feels, what the spacesuit holds — and changing them.

  • Queries: Environment.breathes, airless, localHeat, gravity, weightless; Spacesuit.oxygen, charge, modules.
  • Modifiers (OXYGEN_USE, CLIMATE, GRAVITY) are chained: each listener gets the value of the previous one. GRAVITY runs on both sides — decide only by what both the client and the server see (held item, armor, effects).
  • Places: a sphere of your own gravity, climate rules of a dimension; your own air source block (holdAirSphere, holdRoomAir) keeps air like the mod's Oxygen Dome or Air Blower.
  • Tags: airtight, needs_air, magnetic blocks; pressure_bound, hydrous_minerals items.

Spacesuit modules of addons

Your own torso module in one call: your item fits the four torso slots on the mod's Suit Rack, like the mod's modules, and the suit counts it wherever it counts its own. The item is a plain item of your addon, registered by you.

  • Spec: oxygen, charge (suit capacity), reserve (own store in the module stack: ENERGY or OXYGEN from the rack, or NONE), protects (HEAT, COLD with a share — the mod's Cooling Unit gives 0.4; ACID, RADIATION fully), drain (cost every N ticks while it protects), tick (your action every N ticks, server), hud.
  • Queries: moduleNames, hasModule and moduleCount know "<ns>:<path>"; moduleReserve, moduleReserveCapacity, setModuleReserve (server), protectedFrom(entity, SuitProtection).
  • Events: LifeEvents.MODULE_SPENT (the reserve ran dry; the wearer sees a notice), MODULE_FILLED (the rack filled it up).

Machines: the mod's console and Machine Cards

Package api.machine. Energy, fluids and items of your machine still go through the loaders' standards (Team Reborn Energy and Fabric Transfer, NeoForge capabilities). On top of them you get the mod's console window for your block entity — describe the layout, the mod draws it (sockets, gauge arcs, channels, buttons, readouts, the glass inventory shelves, refusals and hints, Mouse Tweaks, JEI and REI, the Mod window size setting) — and sockets for the mod's Machine Cards with the mod's own multipliers.

  • Consoles.menuType(layout) is a factory: register the menu type in your own registry together with your blocks (Fabric — onInitialize, NeoForge — your DeferredRegister). Open it with Consoles.open(player, machine, menuType) on the server.
  • Your block entity implements ConsoleMachine: a Container plus value(index) (numbers the console shows), pressed(player, button) and title(). Texts are keys of your namespace: gui.<ns>.press.energy, gui.<ns>.press.progress …
  • UpgradeSlots is used twice: in the layout (.upgrades(CARDS)) and in your tick (factor, energyUse).
  • MachineRecipes feeds your items to the Fuel Plant, Pearl Liquefier, Cryo Still and Melt Pump. The Crusher and the Zero-G Forge take data-pack recipes (<mod id>:crushing, <mod id>:crushing_by_tag, <mod id>:zero_g_forging) — no code at all.
  • MachineRecipes.addReactorFeed adds bioreactor feed in code; without code it is data: the item tags <mod id>:oxygen_feed/scant|poor|fair|good|rich|lush or an <mod id>:oxygen_feed recipe.
  • Not open: the mod's resonance network (beams), machine kinds and Mk tiers — your machine joins the network through a mod machine next to it (a Battery Block, an Energy Converter).

Spectra

Package api.spectral. Your own reference (a substance with real lines), sample, light of a sky body, recipe that only an analysis unlocks, ore for the ore scanner and creature weakness — in the same spectral laboratory and spectrograph as the mod's.

  • Lines are real: wavelengths in air from a reference (NIST ASD), strength 3 (main), 2 (strong), 1 (weak). Register references before the samples that use them; the mod's are <mod id>:na, ca, fe, h, co2… (Spectral.references()).
  • Samples burn in the arc (bright lines); gas and ice go under the lamp (absorption()). With reward(...) an identified sample is cracked open by the laboratory.
  • The light of a body is registered under the body's id: your Sky.body, your travel destination, a body of another addon.
  • Every registration is checked like the mod's catalog: no laboratory can take the sample, another reference fits its spectrum, the ruler fits off its place — a WARN line in the log tells what to fix.
  • Queries: knowsReference, hasIdentified, hasMark, progress keys (referenceKey, sourceKey), composition, laboratoryFor, gate; action learn. Events: REFERENCE_LEARNED, IDENTIFIED.
  • Texts: spectrum.<namespace>.ref.<path>[.note], spectrum.<namespace>.source.<path>[.note].

Radio telescope

Package api.radio. Add your own radio sources. A pulsar beats as dashes or ripples on the waterfall and is caught by folding (the player finds its period); a quasar shows hydrogen and hydroxyl lines shifted by its redshift and is caught by the template comb. Place it in galactic (galactic(l, b)) or equatorial (equatorial(ra, dec)) coordinates — the real sky decides where it rises (Earth 40° N, Moon 30° S, orbit sees everything; the dish stops at 12°). By default it is always on the air; daily(from, ticks) and everyDays(n) make a schedule of world time. The first record gives your keys, items and stardust, and can give the spectrograph the light of a body of api.spectral (light) or a navigation record of a place among the stars (navigationRecord). Keep sources 10° apart.

Texts: radio.<ns>.source.<path> (name) and radio.<ns>.source.<path>.found (a line on the record card). Queries: Radio.sources(), signal, foundKey, hasFound, isOnAir, isAboveFloor. "Wow!" and fast radio bursts stay the mod's own.

Open space

Package api.space (API 1.2.0). Bodies in the Solar System, events of open space, whole spaces of your own with passages between them, and places among the stars for the interstellar drive.

  • A body is a ball like the mod's planets: on an orbit, at a point or next to another body (a moon); a surface map, a tilt, a day, an atmosphere rim, rings. It is drawn on the sky, named on the ship's instruments, listed on the system map, starships aim at it, jump to it, fly up to it and stop at its surface. It may stay hidden until found (discovery: the scanner or flying up to it reveals it), lead into the sky of your travel destination (lands) or into another space (passage).
  • A space event is scheduled like a world event in the space dimension (banner, event command) and adds light at its source (glow), a spreading wave (wave) and an action on starships every few ticks; the wave reaches each ship once.
  • A space of your own works like the Solar System: sealed cabins, the starry sky with the balls of its bodies, instruments, scanner, jump drive, autopilot, system map. The dimension is your data (the mod's dimension type solar_system is the simplest). Ships get there through a passage or by rising from the sky of your world whose ball stands there. Light comes from the middle of the space: put your star there.
  • A place among the stars appears on the drive's map «Interstellar» with its navigation record — a photo of your sky sight or a key you grant (recordKey()); the drive jumps there with its own price by light-years; its sky is a nebula of two tones with its own stars. Thirteen places for all addons; the place has no blocks of its own.
  • Starships (Ships): hull, fuel, a blow to the hull, a kick.
  • Queries: Space.bodies, body, nearest, spaces, starPlaces, discovered.
  • Events (SpaceEvents): SPACE_EVENT_STARTED, SPACE_EVENT_ENDED (every event of every space), BODY_DISCOVERED, BODY_REACHED; a passage is the ship leg PASSAGE of TravelEvents.SHIP_TRAVEL.
  • Texts: space_body.<namespace>.<path> with .facts and .near; space.<namespace>.<path>; event.<namespace>.<path>.name|hint; star_place.<namespace>.<path> with .kind, .about, .find, .star.<id>. Surface map — textures/space/<path>.png.
  • Not open yet: anomalies of the codex, far roads, belts and stations of your own.

Starships and stations

api.space also opens the ship's module sockets, the order boards of the peoples' stations and the observatory of your own station.

  • Ship module — Ships.addModule(ctx, ShipModuleSpec.of(itemId)…). The item is yours (register it yourself); it goes into the general module sockets. Multipliers on top of the mod's modules: speed, turn, hull, tank, scanner, jump, jumpCost, shield, and hold(cells); kind(…) makes tiers exclusive, stacks(true) lets several stand together. flight(every, action) runs your code on the server every ≥ 10 ticks while the ship has a pilot. The ship window lists what the module changes under the item's tooltip.
  • Station order — Stations.addOrder(ctx, new StationOrderSpec(path)…): bring(item, count) and/or task((player, takenAt) -> …), people(…), in(space), gate(trust step), reward(supply, emeralds, trust), gift(…), grants(key). Texts: station.<ns>.order.<path> and .goal.
  • Observatory project — Stations.addResearch(ctx, new ResearchSpec(path)…): road, minutes, knows(count, keys…), anomalies(count, ids…), cost(item, count), result(item), recipes(ids…). When done: the key Stations.researchKey(id) and the recipes (the crafting table refuses them before). Texts: research.<ns>.<path>.name|gives|story|data.<n>|data.<n>.hint.
  • Queries and events: Ships.modules(ship), Ships.hasModule, Stations.taken(player), Stations.researched(player, id); SpaceEvents.MODULE_INSTALLED, ORDER_COMPLETED, RESEARCH_COMPLETED.

Starship forms

Since API 1.3.0 an addon adds its own starship — a hull with its own model. You bring the look (a GeckoLib model, texture and animations in your namespace); the mod does the rest: flight over worlds and in space, crossings, jumps, assembly on the shipyard, classes C to S, modules, hold, deck, instruments, autopilot, the call beacon and rescue.

  • Registration — Ships.addForm(ctx, ShipFormSpec.of(path)…) returns <ns>:<path>. road (space, rift, vent: fuel, repair and class upgrades are the road's), role (scout, hauler, explorer, fighter, exotic). Numbers start from one of the mod's forms — like("swift") — and every setter overrides one: size, seat, eye, camera, seats, atmosphere/space flight, hull, tank, hold, jump, scanner, slots, weapons with guns, engine, accent, nozzles, lights, beam, abilities, build.

  • Shipyard — the form appears in the list of its road. node(item, count) sets what it takes, unlock(key…) — after which progress keys the shipyard knows it (otherwise the rule of its role, a blueprint Ships.blueprint(form) or creative mode).

  • Look — your files, by game version (the mod does not move them; an addon for all three versions may ship both layouts at once, the extra one does nothing). The ids in ShipFormSpec stay the same: the mod looks them up in its own GeckoLib layout.

    What26.3 (GeckoLib 5)1.21.1 and 1.20.1 (GeckoLib 4)
    Modelassets/<ns>/geckolib/models/ship/<path>.geo.jsonassets/<ns>/geo/ship/<path>.geo.json
    Animationsassets/<ns>/geckolib/animations/ship/<path>.animation.jsonassets/<ns>/animations/ship/<path>.animation.json
    Textureassets/<ns>/textures/ship/<path>.png (+ <path>_glowmask.png)the same

    The front faces north, bones gear_*, flame_*, light_* and part_0…5 (assembly order) are driven by name. cockpit("swift") borrows a cockpit of the mod around the pilot's eye, deck("swift") — its deck. Name: ship.<ns>.<path>, lore: .lore.

  • Queries: Ships.forms(), isForm, form(ship), knows(player, form), stack(form) — the ship as an item.

  • Closed: own flight physics and own weapon kinds — the form gives numbers, weapon hardpoints and gun muzzles; the guns are the mod's.

Client: graphics, HUD, notices, sounds, moments

Packages api.client (client only, except the registration Hud.add), api.notice, api.sound and api.moment (both sides).

  • Mod graphics — ModGraphics.level(), particles(n), cues(n), post(), layers(n), glow(), far(blocks), shaderPack(): your effects get lighter together with the mod's when the player lowers the setting. Never thin out warnings. Low, Medium and High are presets over knobs the player may tune one by one: preset() is empty for Custom, shake() is the share of camera shake to multiply yours by, cinema() tells whether the camera may leave the player's eyes for a short film (API 1.3.0).
  • HUD — Hud.add(ctx, path, HudZone, order, () -> new MyElement()): each frame your element asks the mod's HUD layout for room and draws where it is placed — never over the mod's elements or the game's (hotbar, chat, boss bars, toasts). Sizes and the slot are in ordinary GUI coordinates; a short form (compactWidth/Height) is used when space is tight. Pass the element as a lambda, not as MyElement::new: then a dedicated server never loads its class.
  • Notices — Notices.send(player, NoticeKind.DONE, text), hold(...) for something that lasts, progress(player, text, done, total) with a bar; from the server or the client. ClientNotices.show(...) from client-only code.
  • Sounds — Sounds.play(level, except, at, source, lead, volume, pitch, layers...): your subtitled lead plus game recordings as layers without their subtitles (Sounds.layer). Sounds.aircraft(ctx, "engine") / atmosphere(ctx, "ambient") put your events under the mod's sound switches.
  • Strong moments — Moments.add(ctx, path, MomentSpec) describes a scene on one time line (columns of light, glowing ground pattern, sky sigil, scene light, flash, shock wave, shake, embers, cinema camera, comm-channel HUD, sounds) with the mod's own looks; Moments.play(level, id, at, ...) on the server sends one small packet, every client plays it by itself.

Open through data

Some of the mod opens without the API, through your addon's ordinary data files (data/<namespace>/…), like vanilla types. Write the mod's ids literally — <mod id>:… (UnboundApi.modId()); fields follow the game version your addon is built for.

  • Structure types (worldgen/structure/<id>.json): <mod id>:settled — a one-template building that stands level and in sight: the type picks the spot, the turn and the height (fields of the game's jigsaw plus max_relief, search, ground, foot — [x, z] points the building stands on, overhead — [x, z, height] points the ground must not reach, level, above, span for a bridge, gorge, upright, sole, cave); <mod id>:settled_village — a jigsaw village whose square looks for flat ground (max_relief, search, radius, above, below, level); <mod id>:deep_dungeon — a many-story dungeon on a grid of cells (layout).
  • Density functions (15) for the noise settings of your dimension, next to vanilla ones. With parameters, for terrain of your own: craters, rays (craters with ejecta rays), facets, honeycomb, iris, ribs, lineae, cells, drift. Without parameters (they repeat the mod's own places): asteroids, wanderer_region, stellar, weave, deep, geode.
  • Machine recipes (recipe/): the Crusher — <mod id>:crushing (ingredient, result, time) and <mod id>:crushing_by_tag (from, to — tag prefixes such as c:raw_materials → c:dusts, count); the Zero-G Forge — <mod id>:zero_g_forging (ingredients — up to four {ingredient, count}, result, time, colors, key — a progress key the recipe needs). JEI and REI show them on the mod's pages by themselves.
  • Dungeon puzzles and the Ringwright niche in your templates — the mod's blocks with their memory in the block entity data of the template: sealed_stone, keystone_socket, rune_plate_1…4 and rune_sign_1…4, beam_emitter, mirror_a/mirror_b, beam_receiver, boss_altar (memory group, order, key, boss — only the mod's bosses, offering, offeringCount; a solved puzzle opens the seals of its group within 48 blocks), dungeon_treasury (loot — a loot table), ringwright_niche (tablet, key — trial waits for Ringwrights.pass, or any progress key).
  • Trades of the mod's villagers and natives are the game's villager_trade and trade_set data (trade_set/<profession>/level_<n>.json, trade_set/native/<people>/<job>/level_<n>.json in the mod's namespace): a data pack replaces a whole file; to add one offer to a native, use Natives.addTrade.
  • The Hydroponic Bed grows any CropBlock or StemBlock — your crop needs nothing registered.
  • The mod's particles, sounds and effects are in the game's registries by id; tags — LifeTags and CreatureTags above.

The mod's catalogs from your files

Nine catalogs of the mod also take entries from plain JSON files in your addon's jar. A file looks like the mod's own catalog, sits at the same path, in your namespace: assets/<namespace>/natives.json (peoples of your own, tasks, trades and topics), spectral.json (standards, samples, light of bodies, recipes after analysis, ores for the scanner, weaknesses, rewards), research.json (observatory projects) and moments.json (strong moments); and the catalogs the mod builds blocks and items from — ores.json, families.json (stone families), flora.json (plants and food), decor.json and gear.json (weapons).

  • The mod reads them itself, once per launch, right after every addon's register, on the server and on the client alike. Entries get the same checks and land in the same lists as the Java calls (Natives.addQuest, Spectral.*, Stations.addResearch, Moments.add), so a file may refer to what your Java code registered. The log line Addon data <namespace>: natives.json 1, … tells how many entries came from each file.
  • Only the namespaces of addons are read — a mod with an UnboundAddon entry point. A data-only addon needs one empty register. Other mods' files with the same name are about their own things and are left alone.
  • Not through data packs. Both sides read these catalogs from their jars and the network carries only ids. A file that only the server had would disagree with the client, which draws the task card, the spectrum window, the observatory and the moment.
  • Ids: a new entry is a path (your namespace is put before it) or <namespace>:path. A reference to a catalog entry works as in the mod's file: without a namespace it is the mod's ("s" is the mod's sulfur, "lunarian" the mod's people), with one it is anyone's (your own is "myaddon:sr"). Items, blocks, creatures and recipes are game ids.
  • A broken entry does not stop the game. The log gets an ERROR Addon <namespace>: <file> — <entry> skipped: <reason>, and the rest of the file still loads.
  • Ores, families, plants and food, decor and weapons are read while the mod registers its blocks, before any register. The mod finds addons without creating their classes, and registers your entries with its own code, in your namespace: the block, its item, the mod's creative tab. Creatures, ships, dungeon sets and armor stay closed to files: they need the mod's GeckoLib models and code. For them, use your own registration plus the API (Species.add, Bosses.register, Ships.addModule, Gear.addArmorSet).

A task in natives.json needs only id and goals. By default, giver is * (any member of the people), repeat is 0 and the texts are keys quest.<namespace>.<people>.<id>.title|brief|offer|waiting|ready|done. Outside the mod the task is <namespace>:<people>/<id>.

research.json holds {"projects": [{"id", "road", "minutes", "color", "result", "recipes", "data", "items"}]}, and moments.json holds {"moments": {"<name>": {"duration": …, "beams": […], …}}}. Both use the same fields as the mod's own files and the Java builders above.

What your addon ships itself

The mod registers the block or item, but its data is yours, in your namespace. The mod's generators only write the mod's own namespace.

  • Blockstates, block and item models (on 26.x items/<path>.json), textures and lang: block.<ns>.<path>, item.<ns>.<path>, and .lore for a description line.
  • Block loot tables (data/<ns>/loot_table/blocks/<path>.json) — without one a block drops nothing.
  • Tags: mineable/pickaxe, needs_iron_tool, c:ores, c:raw_materials/<x>. With the last one, the Crusher's crushing_by_tag crushes your raw ore on its own.
  • Recipes.
  • Ore veins: your configured_feature and placed_feature. To add them to biomes, the mod's ones included, use a NeoForge biome modifier (neoforge:add_features) or Fabric's BiomeModifications.addFeature.
  • On 1.21.1 and 1.20.1, the render layer of plants and glass: render_type in the model on NeoForge and Forge, or BlockRenderLayerMap on Fabric.

An ore's raw and product are new items (a path) or an existing item by full id (minecraft:raw_iron). A weapon uses one of the mod's abilities and mastery, with your own item model. Its texts are gear.<ns>.<path>.role|ability|ability.desc.
