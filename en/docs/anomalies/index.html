Anomalies

The anomaly codex.

The anomaly codex: real phenomena of space — what science knows about them, what they do in the game and where the game took liberties.

Black Hole Merger

gravitational waves crossing the system

The real story. On September 14, 2015, LIGO caught gravitational waves for the first time: black holes of 36 and 29 solar masses merged, and the signal was a "chirp" two tenths of a second long. Space stretched by a thousandth of a proton.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. Waves run through the system; crests come faster and faster. In space a jump started on a crest is free: the drive rides the wave. From the ground the graviscope hears the chirp: click on five crests before the holes merge and it writes a record.

Caught the waveRecorded the chirp

Game liberty: real waves are far too weak to shake a ship.

Blind Spot

a place where light dies

The real story. In 1981 astronomers counting galaxies in Boötes found a hole: a region 330 million light-years across with almost nothing in it — the Boötes Void. Nothing ate those galaxies; matter simply flowed out of the thin places of the young Universe into the dense ones, and the thin places emptied. A void is not a thing — it is where everything left.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.
Kindle a dead hearthClear a Blind Spot

Real voids do not come and go in days, and nothing lives in them. The hearths, the clot and the Shades are the mod's own.

Boötes Void

a patch of sky with almost nothing in it

The real story. In 1981 Robert Kirshner and his colleagues measured galaxy distances in Boötes and found a hole: a region about 330 million light-years across with some sixty galaxies where there should be two thousand. Astronomers joked that if the Milky Way sat in its middle, we would not have learned of other galaxies until the 1960s.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. An astrograph shows the void between Ursa Major and Cygnus. Click it with an imaging plate in your inventory: the dark patch is the first record. When something about the light changes, photograph it again — the second record completes the entry.

The stars came back

Game liberty: the real void is a shortage of galaxies hundreds of millions of light-years deep — no telescope shows it as a dark patch, and it does not change within a human lifetime.

Call of the First

a distant roar below hearing

The real story. Sound below twenty hertz cannot be heard, yet it travels very far: elephants and whales call with it across many kilometers, and the pressure wave of Krakatoa in 1883 went around the Earth several times — barometers all over the world recorded it.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. In the riftscope rings spread from one point of the sky and three notches point at it. Lead the crosshair along all three, one after another, without letting go: three bearings give a direction. It leads into the Interrift, above the disc of the End.

Followed the call to its source

Game liberty: there is no air between the worlds to carry a roar. Something else carries this one.

Spoiler: this describes a place reached late in the game.

The Cold Spot

a place where the echo dies

The real story. In 2004 a Cold Spot was found on the map of the Big Bang's afterglow: a patch in Eridanus colder than chance allows. One explanation is a supervoid — a billion light-years with almost no galaxies in it.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. The ring of a strike goes out where it crosses the spot, and a strike inside it makes no ring at all. Its pulse has a mute beat: two are heard, the third is silence — strike the silence. In the Depths a cooled geode hangs there: rime in the middle of the fire road, dead crystals, and growths of shadow next to which light dims.

Enter the Cold GeodeMine a shadow clot

There are no cold spots in magma. The real Cold Spot is in the sky; what drank the heat here, the game does not say yet.

Dark Matter Cloud

weight without a body

The real story. In the 1970s Vera Rubin measured how galaxies spin: their edges turn as fast as their middles, as if each galaxy sat inside a heavy halo nobody can see. In 2006 the Bullet Cluster showed it plainly: two clusters collided, the hot gas stuck in the middle, and the weight — mapped by the bending of light — flew on.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. A patch of the Main Belt where gravity is strange: the ship drifts sideways toward clumps that are not on the screen — and you can crash into them. A scanner pulse (…) shows them for a few seconds. Fly blind to the core, cut dark grains with the mining beam, find the cache the Ringwrights hid behind the weight.

Blind flightDark grainsThe hidden cache

Game liberty: real dark matter does not gather into rocks — as far as we know it passes through everything, itself included.

The Drifting Seam

a seam that crawls across the sky in one night

The real story. Physicists have a name for a seam in space itself: a cosmic string, a defect thinner than a proton that the young Universe may have left stretched across the whole sky. Telescopes have looked for their double images for decades. So far, not one.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. Once every ten days a zigzag seam crawls across the sky of the End. Tear it on the move, like the seam of a rift. While it is open, a Pocket stands in the Interrift — a small island with a cache. When the day ends the seam closes and the Pocket melts with everything left on it.

Opened a Pocket cache

Game liberty: nobody has seen a cosmic string, let alone sewn an island into one.

Fast Radio Burst

an arc of dispersion

The real story. In 2007 Duncan Lorimer found in old records a radio pulse of a few milliseconds, brighter than anything — and from beyond the Galaxy. Plasma on the way delays low frequencies: the delay measures the road. On April 28, 2020, such a burst first came from our own Galaxy, from the magnetar SGR 1935+2154 in Vulpecula.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. A burst comes once a day at a random moment; every fifth day, a series from Vulpecula. The receiver keeps the last burst in its buffer for two minutes: its lamp blinks gold and clicks. Dedispersion: turn the wheel until the arc straightens into a line, and record. The pulse charges two batteries.

repeating source in Vulpecula

Game liberty: across this band a real arc takes a couple of seconds — here it is stretched by half, and no burst would charge a battery.

The Fifth Echo

an echo where there is no vent: something hollow lies below

The real story. In 1936 the Danish seismologist Inge Lehmann noticed faint earthquake waves where there should have been none — in the "shadow" of the Earth's core. They had bounced off something inside it: that is how the solid inner core was found. A source is located the same way to this day: three stations, three distances, one point.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. A strike wakes an echo with no vent behind it, and it comes back double — from the near wall and from deep inside. Three strikes from different places leave three arcs; where they cross lies a hollow world of the Depths, the Nether Sun. Once you have heard it, a depth ship may dive into the crack of its shell.

Land insideSit out a flare in the shadeMine suncoal

There is no hollow world with a sun inside under the Earth. The idea is Edmond Halley's (1692): he proposed a hollow Earth with a glowing core to explain the wandering compass — beautiful, and wrong.

Gamma-Ray Burst

the brightest flash in the universe

The real story. Gamma-ray bursts were found in 1967 by the Vela satellites watching for nuclear tests. GRB 221009A of October 9, 2022, the brightest ever recorded, reached Earth's ionosphere from two billion light-years away.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. A point in the sky flickers and the count begins: in a minute the wave passes through the whole system. Get behind a planet or a moon — the instruments show which side is shadow.

The Heartbeat

a slow double beat below all the vents

The real story. In 1962 the geophysicist Jack Oliver found that the Earth has a pulse: a faint tremor every 26 seconds, coming from the Gulf of Guinea. Seismographs all over the world hear it; what beats there is still argued about.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. Below every vent something beats twice and falls silent. Each beat sends a pale wave across the whole magma: follow the waves to their source. Strike both halves of the beat. Three seismograms on three different days, and the Heartbeat is tracked: it leads to the threshold of the Crucible at the bottom of the Depths.

Read the threshold plaque

The real pulse of the Earth is a single tremor every 26 seconds; the double beat is a liberty of the game.

The Knot

a dark place the void sparks fly around

The real story. Real skies have such places too: Barnard 68 is a cloud of cold dust about five hundred light-years away, half a light-year across and so dense that not a single star shines through it. For a century it was taken for a hole in the sky.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. In the riftscope the sparks that fly to the rifts bend around an empty spot: lead the crosshair around its rim with the button held. In the Interrift it is a blot two thousand blocks wide beyond the Facet where a ship's light dies. Ship scanner pulses inside it take seam casts; in its middle something is hidden.

Stood at the Threshold

Game liberty: the Knot is not dust. It is the same dark stuff as the cloud in the Main Belt.

Magnetar in Vulpecula

a star that shouts in radio — again and again

The real story. SGR 1935+2154: a neutron star with a field of 10¹⁴ gauss, some 30,000 light-years away. On April 28, 2020, CHIME and STARE2 caught a millisecond radio burst from it — the first fast radio burst known to come from inside our Galaxy. On December 27, 2004, a giant flare of another magnetar, SGR 1806-20, reached Earth from fifty thousand light-years away and shook its ionosphere.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. Once every five days its bursts come in a series of three to six in a night. Catch them with a radio telescope, its dish on Vulpecula: three bursts are a navigation record for the interstellar drive (the Quasar stage). There, by the star, the field jams modules without a Magnetic Shield, and every two minutes a starquake wave crosses the whole place.

Survived a starquake

Game liberty: real starquakes come years apart, not every two minutes; the torn shell here is the crust of the star, while around the real SGR 1935+2154 lies a supernova remnant.

Spoiler: this describes a place reached late in the game.

Planet Nine

a primordial black hole beyond the Kuiper Belt

The real story. In 2016 Konstantin Batygin and Mike Brown noticed that the orbits of the farthest Kuiper Belt bodies are bunched together, as if an unseen planet of 5–10 Earth masses herds them. In 2019 Jakub Scholtz and James Unwin suggested it may be a primordial black hole as old as the Big Bang and the size of a grapefruit. It is still being searched for.

Nothing comes back from beyond a real horizon; a ring singularity is what the Kerr solution of 1963 predicts.

Pulsars

clocks of the sky

The real story. In 1967 Jocelyn Bell noticed "a bit of scruff" on a chart: a pulse every 1.3373 seconds. It was jokingly called LGM-1 — "little green men". It was a neutron star: a sun squeezed into a city, turning a radio beam like a lighthouse. The Crab pulsar spins thirty times a second; ships can be steered by such clocks (SEXTANT, 2018), and the Pioneer plaque marks our home by fourteen of them.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. Folding: the wheel changes the trial period. Far off, the raster is noise; closer, slanted streaks; exact, a column. Each timed pulsar makes your ship's jump 6% cheaper; all three are a Pulsar Navigator: a quarter cheaper, and the telescope builds the device itself — the heart of Interstellar Drive II. The Vela pulsar is seen only from the Moon or from orbit.

Game liberty: a real fold takes minutes of data, and the Crab was found by its giant pulses.

Quasar 3C 273

lines out of place

The real story. In 1963 Maarten Schmidt looked at the spectrum of the "radio star" 3C 273 and suddenly recognized in four strange lines plain hydrogen — shifted to the red by 15.8%. The "star" proved to be the core of a galaxy 2.4 billion light-years away, brighter than a trillion suns. Quasars are so far away that they stand still: the coordinate frame of the whole sky (ICRF) is built on them.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. Template: drag the comb of hydrogen (1420 MHz) and hydroxyl (1665 and 1667) left until its teeth sit on the faint lines — the lines show only when the dish has gathered the signal. One record a day; three records are a Quasar Reference, the heart of Interstellar Drive III. And use a spectrograph on the telescope: the quasar's light goes to the spectral laboratory.

redshift z = 0.158light taken by spectrographquasar reference

Game liberty: the hydrogen and hydroxyl of 3C 273 itself are too faint for a dish this size.

Coronal Mass Ejection

a storm of plasma from the Sun

The real story. A flare can throw a billion tonnes of magnetized plasma into space. The Carrington event of 1859 lit auroras as far as the Caribbean and sparked telegraph lines; the G5 storm of May 2024 brought auroras to Moscow and Mexico.

In the game. A flare on the Sun, then a shock front races outward. In open space it cracks the hull and throws the ship aside; hide in the shadow of a planet or moon. A sail rides the front instead. Where it passes, magnetized planets glow with auroras — on Earth too.

Solar Corona

a million-degree plasma above the surface

The real story. The corona is hotter than the surface beneath it — why is still the "coronal heating problem". The Parker Solar Probe was the first to fly through it in 2021, and in 2024 it passed 6.1 million km from the Sun at 690,000 km/h, the fastest object ever built.

Supernova

a dying giant brighter than the Moon

The real story. In 1054 Chinese astronomers saw a "guest star" in daylight for 23 days — its remnant is the Crab Nebula with a pulsar spinning 30 times a second. Betelgeuse, a red supergiant, will explode "soon" — within 100,000 years. Gold, platinum and uranium are born in the r-process of such explosions and of neutron-star mergers (kilonova GW170817, 2017).

In the game. A new star flares in Cassiopeia, visible by day. While it shines, aim at it in space and jump along its light: at the edge of the system an echo of the remnant gathers — mine r-process ore and fly between the pulsar beams.

Tabby's Star

a star that dims for no reason

The real story. KIC 8462852 in Cygnus, 1,470 light-years away. In the Kepler data it dimmed unevenly — by up to 22% — in dips no planet could make. Tabetha Boyajian's 2015 paper was called "Where's the Flux?"; the guess of an alien swarm of collectors went round the world. Most likely it is dust.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. The graviscope sees it in Cygnus, but a dip shows only through a coronagraph in your inventory. On one or two nights out of five its light sinks for a few minutes in the middle of the night: click on the star at the bottom of the dip. Three dips make a light curve — and in it the interstellar drive finds the way.

The light curve

Game liberty: the real dips came weeks apart and lasted days; here they come at night and last minutes.

The Visitor

a body from another star, passing through

The real story. On October 19, 2017, the Pan-STARRS telescope caught a body flying too fast to belong to the Sun: 1I/ʻOumuamua, "a messenger from afar arriving first". Long like a cigar or flat like a pancake, it tumbled, and it sped up a little more than gravity allowed — which is where the guess about a light sail came from. After it came 2I/Borisov (2019) and 3I/ATLAS (2025).

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. Every seven to ten days a body from another star crosses the inner system in a single day. Catch up with it, match its speed, cut samples with the mining beam: it never comes back. In the rock there are pockets of ice older than the Sun. Every third Visitor is not a rock.

Void Tide

the sky grows murky a day before the wave

The real story. A tide is the pull of a neighbor: the Moon and the Sun stretch the oceans, and when they stand in one line the water rises highest. In the Bay of Fundy the sea goes up and down by sixteen meters twice a day.

The Wanderer

a planet without a star

The real story. Planets thrown out of their systems drift between the stars in the dark. They are found by microlensing (the OGLE and MOA surveys): when such a planet passes in front of a far star, its gravity gathers the light and the star flares for a day or two. In 2023 the Webb telescope found dozens of such worlds in the Orion Nebula, many of them in pairs (JuMBO).

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. Every ten to fourteen days the Wanderer stops in the Kuiper Belt for two days, each time in a new place. It gives no light, so it is found the way real ones are: an astrograph shows a background star that flares up and fades. Click at the peak of the flare; three nights of watching give its path, and the almanac below names the hour of the pass and marks it for your ship. In space it is a black disc against the stars; the scanner sees it. Land before it leaves: under the ice lie warm lakes and the ruins of the Orphans. Whoever stays when it goes is thrown off.

Game liberty: a rogue planet would cross the Solar System once in millions of years, not every two weeks, and it would not stop.

The Wandering Heat

a hot spot crawling under the crust

The real story. In 1963 the Canadian J. Tuzo Wilson explained the Hawaiian chain: a plate creeps over a hot spot deep in the mantle, and island after island is burnt through it. The same November the island of Surtsey rose out of the sea off Iceland; the island of Hunga Tonga was born in 2015 and blown apart in 2022.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. The spot is seen plainly, but it will not stay: keep your aim on it and strike to its quick beat — let it slip, and the hits melt away. Where the heat stops, once every eight to twelve days an island-bubble rises in the Depths for a day — the Rising — and then sinks.

In truth it is not the hot spot that wanders but the plate above it; here the heat itself is on the move.

Wormhole

a mouth that opens for half an hour

The real story. In 1935 Einstein and Rosen found a bridge between two sheets of space in the equations of gravity. In 1988 Morris and Thorne showed what a traversable hole would need: exotic matter with negative energy to hold the throat open. Kip Thorne later advised the film "Interstellar", and its wormhole is a sphere for the same reason as here: a hole in three dimensions is a ball, not a ring.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. Every three to five days a mouth opens in the Solar System for half an hour: a ball with the sky of another place inside. Fly into it and you come out at the farthest place you have already been to — even at another star, if your drive once took you there; the hole never opens the unknown. In the throat a bubble of exotic matter drifts: hold your aim on it.

Went throughCaught the bubble

Game liberty: nobody has seen exotic matter in such amounts, and nobody knows whether a wormhole can exist at all.

The "Wow!" Signal

72 seconds on the hydrogen line

The real story. On August 15, 1977, the Big Ear radio telescope in Ohio heard a narrow signal on the hydrogen line, 1420 MHz, from Sagittarius for 72 seconds — exactly as long as a point of sky takes to drift through its beam. Jerry Ehman circled the printout "6EQUJ5" and wrote "Wow!". It was never heard again.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.

In the game. Here it repeats — every sidereal day, 66 ticks earlier each day, like the stars. Narrow band: keep the sight on the drifting trace for all 72 seconds and notch away the carriers creeping toward it. Four clean readings out of six make a record; six make "6EQUJ5". Record it from Earth, the Moon and orbit: three bearings cross.

bearing taken6EQUJ5: six clean readings

Game liberty: the real signal came once. And the bearing here points not to Sagittarius, but much closer — beyond Neptune: something there repeats a voice that is not its own.
