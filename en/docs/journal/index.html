Journal and progression

The star journal leads you through the mod: chapters are branches of the path, stages are goals.

The star journal leads you through the mod: chapters are branches of the path, stages are goals. Here are the open stages and what to do for them; hidden stages (sky discoveries, worlds, mysteries) the journal reveals itself when the time comes.

Open the star journal — the map with every stage, as in the game

Start

Star JournalOpen the journal.

This journal records everything you learn about the sky. New pages appear on their own as soon as you discover something. The way to other worlds begins with a simple question: what is up there? If the journal is lost, a new one can be made from a book and lapis lazuli: the records are kept not in it but in your memory, and the new journal will open on the same page.

OpticsMake a polished lens. Mix sand, an amethyst shard and clay into glass blend and smelt it into optical glass. Four pieces make a lens blank. Polish the blank with grinding paste and a piece of wool.

Ordinary glass is cloudy and bends light unevenly. Optical glass is made from a pure blend, and the lens is ground by hand until it gathers light into a single point.

Refractor TelescopeBuild a telescope from four parts: an optical tube, an eyepiece, a mount head and a tripod. The tube and the eyepiece each need a polished lens; tubes and gears are made from copper sheets.

A refractor gathers light with a lens at the front of the tube. Galileo looked at the sky through one just like it — and saw mountains on the Moon.

First LightPlace the telescope under the open sky and look through it at night.

Astronomers call the first look through a new telescope its first light. Bright stars of the same color often belong to one constellation: select a star and then another to draw a line between them. If the figure does not come together, the telescope will find one missing line for you in exchange for stardust. Stardust comes from shooting stars: aim at one and left-click before it burns out. Every discovery in the sky brings some as well.

Ursa MajorFind seven bluish stars in the shape of a dipper and connect them: the four stars of the bowl into a closed quadrangle, the three stars of the handle into a chain leading away from it.

The best-known figure of the northern sky. The two stars at the edge of the dipper point to the North Star, and Mizar in the handle has been used to test eyesight since ancient times.

Village CraftsTrade with a villager of a new craft. Earth's villages now have houses of a new kind — an observatory, a rocket workshop, a fuel depot, a power house, a greenhouse — and a jobless villager takes the craft of the workplace beside him: an astronomer at a telescope, a rocket engineer at a drafting table, a fuel technician at a fuel plant, a power engineer at a charging station, a hydroponist at a hydroponic bed.

The specialists of the villages save long chains of crafting, but not the journey: the astronomer sells optical glass, lens blanks and polished lenses, later stardust and a primary mirror; the rocket engineer sells hull plates, heat tiles, nozzles, turbopumps and avionics; the fuel technician sells canisters of fuel and, at the higher levels, of helium-3; the power engineer sells charged batteries, and as a master a charging station; the hydroponist sells food and a hydroponic bed. They pay emeralds for glass, amethyst, copper, coal and even lunar regolith.

And 6 hidden stages: the journal reveals them itself.

Reflector

Reflector TelescopeBuild a reflector: an eyepiece, a truss tube, a primary mirror and an equatorial mount are added to the old refractor. The mirror is made of two lens blanks and coated with mirror coating.

A mirror is easier to make large than a lens, and the larger it is, the fainter the stars it shows. The first telescope of this kind was built by Isaac Newton.

And 3 hidden stages: the journal reveals them itself.

Astrograph

AstrographMake the astrograph: a moonstone lens, an imaging plate and a star tracker are added to the reflector. It is too heavy for a tripod. Lay a base of nine plates, put a pier in the middle and the astrograph on top.

The sky turns, and stars drift out of view. The tracker turns the telescope after them, and the plate gathers light for hours — this is how things the eye cannot see are revealed.

And 3 hidden stages: the journal reveals them itself.

Graviscope

GraviscopeMake the graviscope: two resonance rings and a gravity core are added to the astrograph. Take the astrograph off its pier (the base stays): the graviscope stands on the same base and pier, with four gravity pylons at the corners.

Some things give off no light at all, but anything that has mass bends space around itself. The graviscope sees these bends — which means it sees what cannot be seen.

And 1 hidden stage: the journal reveals them itself.

Vents

MagmascopeMake the magmascope: a heatproof lens, a deep bell and a magma core on a blackstone base. In the Nether, lay a three-by-three slab of scorched plating, put the magmascope in the middle and heatproof piers two blocks tall on the corners. When the structure assembles, pour three buckets of lava into the bowl.

The Nether has no sky: there is stone overhead. But underfoot, beneath the floor of the lava seas, lies bottomless magma, and there are hollows in it — vents with other worlds at the bottom. Magma lets no light through, and eyes find nothing in it. The magmascope strikes it with a bell and listens: where it is hollow below, the strike comes back as an echo. Fire bubbles rise through the magma: when one passes through the sight, click to catch a deep ember; finding a vent brings four. Three embers buy the toll (the hint key): it shows the nearest vent. Deep embers pay the gates of the worlds below the vents.

And 4 hidden stages: the journal reveals them itself.

Rifts

RiftscopeMake the riftscope: two resonance rings, a void lens, two End resonators and a rift core in a purpur frame. In the End, build it a five-by-five base of void plating with a rift socket in the middle of each side and a two-block void pier; the riftscope goes on top. When the structure assembles, set an eye of ender into each of the four sockets.

The sky of the End is not a sky. It is a thin film of void, and there are tears in it: behind each, another world. The riftscope gathers no light — its four eyes of ender look where all endermen look and show where the film is thinnest. Sparks of the void drift toward the rifts on their own: follow them. Cracks open in the void now and then: aim at one and click before it closes for a void shard; finding a rift brings four. Three shards buy the call (the hint key): it shows the nearest rift and sends every spark to it. Void shards also spin the thread of the shuttle and pay the gates of the worlds beyond the rifts.

And 4 hidden stages: the journal reveals them itself.

Rockets

Rocket FuelBuild a fuel plant and fill a canister. Open the plant, put in coal and empty canisters. Two pieces of coal fill one canister. Hoppers work with the plant too.

Liquid fuel has been made from coal since the 1920s: coal is turned into gas, and the gas into kerosene. The same plant extracts helium-3 from lunar regolith — four blocks per canister — and from the haze of the gas giants. The solar wind has been bringing it to the Moon for billions of years; fusion engines run on it.

And 6 hidden stages: the journal reveals them itself.

Frontier

NativesTalk to the natives of another world: the Lunarians live on the Moon, in the Lunar Court on the dark plains of the maria — round stone domes half sunk in the regolith, a mirror bowl with the blue reflection of the Earth, lamps of moonstone. Click one of them: the talkative ones answer, the rest show their wares at once, unless they have a task for you. Answers are chosen with the mouse or keys 1–9. A rocket with a surface scanner names the nearest settlement as soon as it lands.

Each of the sixteen worlds reached by rocket, shuttle or bell has its own people: the Martians build tall red towers with windcatchers, the Belt Riders hover over the clouds of Jupiter and never touch the ground, the Comb Keepers slam their lids and hide when struck, the Fruiting Folk are fruit of the Mycelium itself. They remember who you are: what you have asked, what they have given you, which master you have beaten. Ask a Frostwalker about the crystals on its back, and it will give you a diamond: to them it is only weather. Every elder sells a chart to the dungeon of their world, and most of them later sell what its gate or altar asks for. They trade like villagers, for emeralds and the currency of their world, and the stock is renewed every day.

And 11 hidden stages: the journal reveals them itself.

Space

ShipyardMake a shipyard console from the heart of the master who ends the first worlds of a road — the Terminator Warden of Mercury, the Mother of the Comb of the Hive or the Giant's Remnant of the Ash — and set it at the middle of a long side of a slipway of nine by five plates, with two crane towers of four sections at opposite corners. Click the console: it shows with lights what is still missing.

A complete shipyard lights up and becomes one great machine: cranes over the slipway, guide lights along its edges, a hologram of a ship over the console. It builds ships whose blueprints you know — the scout of a road is known after that master, the hauler after the last master of the road; the rest are drawn on blueprints from space caches and stations — and raises a ship on the slipway to the next class. It also takes a ship apart: hold the Take apart button, and its modules, hold, cockpit, core and part of its hull sections and drives go to the shipyard store; fuel drains into empty buckets and canisters from your inventory.

And 72 hidden stages: the journal reveals them itself.

The Base

Resonance GridJoin two machines with a beam: click one with the grid tuning fork, then the other. A beam needs a clear line: a block in the way breaks the beam until it is removed, but glass lets it through. Mk1 lenses reach 24 blocks, Mk4 lenses 48; for more, put a grid pole or a resonator between them, and lead the beam into a sealed room through a lens bushing. A sneak-click with the fork puts beams out.

Energy runs along a beam as pulses of light, in the direction of flow: the more current, the faster and brighter they run, and an idle beam is dim. The crystal of a resonator sets its reach — quartz 24, moonstone 32, fire opal 40, methane ice 48: click the resonator with a new crystal. Cheaper and shorter: a beam mirror turns a beam round a corner, a beam prism splits it in three.

Entangled PairMake a pair of entangled resonators, put them where you like — at the Earth base and at the Moon base, say — and join each to its grid with a beam. Then click one with the tuning fork, then the other: the pair is entangled.

The pair shares one store of energy: what one end takes from its grid, the other gives to its own, at any distance and between worlds. A double spiral of light rises from each end into the sky: sparks climb it where energy leaves and fall where it arrives.

First StandardRecord your first standard. 1. Click the laboratory with a simple sample: ice, a bone, an ingot of iron or lead (the tooltip of such an item says "Sample for the spectral laboratory"). 2. When the rainbow lights up on its screen, click again: the spectrum opens. 3. Click every line and press "Record a new standard". After that, mixtures: pick standards below and drag the ruler until every tooth stands on a line.

The scale of every sample is off by its own amount, so the ruler has to be found by the pattern of the lines. What no standard explains is something new — unless there are two new things at once: then find a simpler sample. Some recipes open only to those who know what a thing is made of.

SpectrographMake a spectrograph. By day, click a telescope with it to catch the light of the Sun; at night, while observing, right-click a planet you have found.

Light carries the lines of everything it passed through. The air of a world is written in its spectrum: you can know what you will breathe there before you fly.

HeliumFind the line that belongs to nothing on Earth: study the light of the Sun. You will need the standards of hydrogen, sodium, calcium and iron.

A yellow line a hair from the sodium pair. Janssen and Lockyer saw it in 1868 and named the element after the Sun; on Earth helium was found only twenty-seven years later.

Signs of LifeFind a world whose light has oxygen next to methane: take the spectrum of a planet with the spectrograph through a telescope. You know this world better than any other — but you cannot see it from its own ground.

Oxygen and methane destroy each other: where both remain, something keeps making them. This is the sign astronomers look for in the light of distant worlds.

Ore ScannerBuild an ore scanner. A click sends a pulse: it shows nearby ores that hold the substance of a standard you have recorded. Sneak-click picks the standard. The charge is redstone dust from your inventory.

The scanner does not look for "ore in general" — it looks for lines it knows: what your handbook lacks, it cannot see. It finds lead in galena too, and sulfur in cinnabar.

Unknown SampleFind an unknown mineral, a strange alloy or alien dust — in a ruin chest, in a shipwreck, on the master of a world — and identify it in the laboratory. An identified sample opens: there is an item inside.

The mineral hides a gem, the alloy can be repeated, and the dust is older than the Sun. Which is which, only the lines will tell.

Weak SpotIdentify a part of a creature in the laboratory: a gland, a core, a plate, marrow. Whoever knows what a creature is made of hits it harder.

Gypsum splits along its layers, old bone is porous, glass is full of stress. The gain is small, but in a long fight with the master of a world it shows.

Complete HandbookRecord every standard of the handbook of spectra: the ores of all worlds, the light of the Sun and of the anomalies, the unknown samples. The laboratory pays stardust for each new standard, and richly for the whole book.

A table with no empty cells left: everything your worlds are made of is called by its name.

SpacesuitMake all four parts of the spacesuit and put them on at a dressing table. It protects only as a full set. A new torso is empty: open an oxygen tank while wearing the suit, and it fills it.

The torso holds five minutes of air on its own; bottles add more. Modules go into its four sockets on a suit rack. An oxygen tank and a suit rack fill the suit right on you: open them while wearing it. The torso also refills itself wherever there is air: under a dome, in a sealed room, by a rocket with life support.

Dressing TableBuild a dressing table: the spacesuit is put on and taken off only there. Open it and put the four parts into the sockets.

The suit goes on under armor and takes none of its slots, so any armor can be worn on top of it. Without the table the suit cannot be put on at all.

Hydroponic BedMake a hydroponic bed and plant seeds on top of it, as on farmland: wheat, carrots, potatoes, beetroot, melons or pumpkins. It needs no water, no soil and no air, and lights the sprout itself.

Roots stand in a nutrient solution instead of soil, so the bed works on the Moon as well as at home, and the crop grows about twice as fast. Off Earth it is the only way to farm: ordinary farmland grows nothing there, even under a dome. In Earth's villages the hydroponist works at such a bed and sells bread, apples, golden carrots and the beds themselves.

StorageBuild a storage terminal and storage cells and stand the cells right against the terminal and each other. Give the terminal a beam and open it: everything in the cells is in one window, with search and sorting.

Click takes a stack, right-click half, Shift puts it in your inventory; Shift-click in your inventory stores a stack. A pneumatic station "On request" against the storage sends orders through the tubes: Ctrl+click an item (Cmd on a Mac). A broken cell keeps its contents.

Auto-AssemblerBuild an auto-assembler and a recipe card. Take the card in one hand and a sample of a part in the other and use the card: it remembers the crafting recipe. Put the card into the assembler and the parts into its tray.

A pneumatic station "On request" beside the assembler brings missing parts from a storage with another such station. A hopper below takes the finished parts. Several recipes for one item: use the card again.

Liquids and BucketsFill a bucket with a liquid. An empty bucket is filled by clicking a machine that gives the liquid — a fuel plant, a fuel depot, a pearl liquefier, a cryo still; the molten gold of the Forge and the tears of the Lament are scooped right from the sea and the lakes. A bucket is the same as a canister, a thousand units: click a craft or a piped machine with it to pour it in.

Spilled liquids live their own life. Molten gold burns like lava and sets into slag in water; tears hold you like water but bring sorrow — slowness and weakness; rocket fuel burns, and lava sets it alight; helium-3 boils away in seconds; liquid pearl throws whoever touches it aside, like a chorus fruit; coolant freezes and ices the water nearby. A dispenser pours buckets by itself, and the tanks and pipes of other mods know our liquids.

Airlock DoorMake an airlock door and set it into the wall of a sealed room: two blocks wide and three tall, it slides apart at a click and closes by itself three seconds later. While closed, it holds the air.

Every full block holds air, and so do glass panes and pipes; an ordinary door does not, and a room behind it is never sealed. Two airlock doors with a lobby between them make a real airlock: open them one at a time, and the room keeps its air.

Machine UpgradesRaise a machine to the next version or make an upgrade card. The next version is made of the previous one, a machine core and two hull plates. The Mk2 core takes moonstone from the Moon; the Mk3 core, the metal frost of Venus and the fire opal of Mars; the Mk4 core, the golden sulfide of Mercury and methane ice from the bergs of Uranus. A core is tuned with the dust of a road: Mk2 with stardust, Mk3 with deep embers, Mk4 with void shards.

Every version is twice as strong and roomy as the one before — Mk4 is eight times the first — and has one more socket for cards in its panel. Nothing is free: the Accelerator works half as fast again but eats two thirds more energy; the Economizer saves a sixth; the Field Amplifier widens a dome or a field by a quarter; the Expander adds half again to the capacity; the Catalyst squeezes a quarter more out of fuel and kelp; the Membrane closes a fifth of the leak; the Reflector gives a solar panel a third more light; the Conductor lets half as much again through a battery or a tank. A card fits only where it has work to do.

InstrumentsMake a multimeter out of a copper coil and click a machine with it: it shows what the beam grid gives, asks for and stores. A manometer out of a valve does the same for oxygen and fuel pipes.

Spare parts are not dead weight: a hull plate in one hand patches worn gear in the other, a copper sheet patches a little, and the crusher gives back part of the metal of any part.

LiftBuild a lift drive and guide rails. Put the drive at the bottom of a shaft three blocks wide, facing the shaft — the cabin runs in front of its face — and stack the rails on it: the cabin climbs as high as the stack. Put a call post on every floor within five blocks of the middle of the shaft, give the drive a beam and click a post.

In the cabin a jump takes you a floor up and a sneak a floor down; a click on its floor opens the list. Calls from several floors wait their turn. If the power fails between floors, the cabin holds on its brakes: sneak to let it creep down to the floor below. A higher mark is faster and climbs higher.

Jump BeaconBuild two jump beacons and put one at each of your bases in the same world. Give both a beam, stand on one, click it and choose the other in the list — then stay on the beacon until the jump has charged.

A jump costs charge by distance, and only you go, with what you carry. Sneak-click opens the panel: there the owner lets guests in. Between worlds beacons do not jump: that is what gates, rockets and ships are for.

Helper DroneBuild a drone nest and a route card. Sneak-click a chest with the card for "from", then another chest or a machine for "to". Put the card into the nest and give the nest a beam.

A sample in the other hand, when you use the card, tells the drone what to carry. The drone flies straight or arches over an obstacle, takes only what the other end has room for, and charges in the nest. It is slow: where a tube reaches, pneumatic mail is better.

Suit ModulesMake a suit module and fit it into the torso on a suit rack. The Moon is hot by day and freezing by night: a cooling unit and a heating unit hold that off while the suit has charge.

Four sockets, nine modules: oxygen bottles for ten and twenty minutes, an air recycler that saves a third of the oxygen, a cooling unit, a heating unit, a power cell of 8000 ⚡, a solar film that charges in daylight, an antigravity unit for heavy worlds and an acid-proof coating for Venus and the Breath, which needs no charge. Venus wants the cooling unit and the coating, the giants the heating unit, Jupiter the antigravity.

KnapsackMake a knapsack. Open it with its key (F9), or use it from your hand. With a slot mod (Trinkets; on NeoForge also Curios) it is worn in the back socket; without one the knapsack works right from your inventory.

Cards go into its sockets: "Pickup" sends what you pick up to the same items in it, "Feed" refills your hand as you build, a small suit tank or a power cell keeps a reserve for the suit. A higher mark has more cells and sockets and keeps what was inside.

Ringwrights

Ringwright SignsWhoever set the gate rings left writing beside them. Find a gate ring and click the stele of dark stone with carved signs on its dais. On Earth the ring stands on a dais of deepslate, often far away: a village astronomer sells a chart to it.

Each sign is a whole word. Touch an inscription and the stone shows you a fresco: what is in it is what the words are about. Pick a sign, then a picture you have met — when every sign of the phrase is right, the phrase comes together. The lexicon tab of the journal keeps everything you have learned.
