Mod settings

“Mod graphics” — low, medium, high presets and fine knobs — and other settings.

Settings open with the gear in the journal’s header, from the game’s graphics settings or from the mod list (on Fabric via Mod Menu, on NeoForge with the Config button).

Presets and knobs

Low, Medium and High are presets: each sets every knob below at once. Change any knob and the preset becomes Custom; pick a preset again to set every knob back. On the right, the same live scene follows your knobs. Click a step to choose it, click elsewhere on the row for the next step (right click — back) or use the mouse wheel; the hint under the cursor says what the knob changes and what it costs.

"More" and "Further" go past what the effects were designed for, so only a knob gives them, never a preset.

Two comfort knobs are left alone by the presets — they are about how you like to watch, not about the frame rate: Camera shake (off, weak, as designed: moonquakes, the saccade of the Eye, the blow of a strong moment, a ship in flight, a meteorite impact, a rocket on takeoff and on entering an atmosphere) and Cinema of strong moments (off: you watch the gates wake or a world master come out with your own eyes; the shelter from damage stays). No knob hides a danger sign: attack warnings, impact spots and field edges are drawn at any setting, and every camera shake has its own sign besides it.

Effects with shader packs is a switch in the mod settings. With a shader pack (Iris, Oculus) the mod's post-effects turn off by themselves; switch it on and they run with the pack too, at your own risk: the pack may spoil them (a black screen or streaks: switch it off again). Tested with Complementary and Bliss.

Mod window size

The mod's screens (the journal, machine consoles, the storage terminal and others) are drawn in finer detail than the game's own, and their small text is smaller than the game's letters. If they look too small at the GUI Scale that suits the game's screens, the mod settings have a choice on the right of the Mod graphics line:

  • Match game (the default) — the mod's screens follow GUI Scale, like the game's own;
  • Larger — halfway to the largest, whatever GUI Scale is set to;
  • Largest — always the largest that fits the game window, as with “GUI Scale: Auto”.

The mod's screens are always drawn in whole screen pixels (in-between sizes would look blurry), so the size is rounded to the nearest step: on a 1920×1080 screen at GUI Scale 2 they are drawn pixel for pixel and Larger and Largest are twice as large, at GUI Scale 3 and up the three are the same; at 1366×768 the three are the same. The choice takes effect at once, even on an open screen; the game's screens and the mod's HUD do not change.

The whole screen grows, your inventory included: in machine consoles, the storage terminal and ship windows its slots, items and stack counts on a 1920×1080 screen are as large as in the game's own inventory at GUI Scale 2 when Match game is set at GUI Scale 2, and as at GUI Scale 4 otherwise.

Auto-focus search on open

A switch under Mod window size, off by default. Off, the storage terminal opens with its search box unfocused, so the inventory key (E) closes it like any container; to search, click the box or press / or Tab (Ctrl+F works too). On, letters go straight into the search as soon as the terminal opens, but E then types into the search instead of closing the window (Esc clears the search first, then closes). The setting is yours alone: it is stored on your computer, not on the server.

Clicking anywhere outside the box (a stored item, a slot, a button) releases the search, so letters act as window keys again and E closes the terminal. Back in the box (click, / or Tab), the query is selected and the first letter you type starts a new one, as in the creative inventory search; the arrow keys clear the selection.

Mod sounds

The mod plays the game's own recordings: each action has its own set, pitch and rhythm, and every ship has its own takeoff, landing and maneuver sounds. The synthesized "Own sounds" are gone from the mod. Working machines hum (the four nearest within sixteen blocks are heard), a ship's afterburner has a roar of its own, and a lair guardian waking or a gate crossing is marked by a sound.

The mod settings have two switches above the Done button, both on by default:

  • Aircraft sounds: the ships' own takeoff, landing and maneuver sounds; off, a ship plays the older shared recordings;
  • Ambience sounds: the sound backdrop of the worlds (below); off, it falls silent at once.

With the game's subtitles on, the mod's actions carry captions of their own: "The Blind Spot clot draws breath", "The Wayfarer passes a corridor gate", "The light is whole again": one line per action, not the names of the recordings it is built from.

World ambience

Every world of the mod has its own soundscape — not music, but the place itself. For example, the muffled steady wind of Mars (darker and slower at night) and the rolling steam of the Breath. Each biome adds its own layer: an airy drone with a distant shimmer on the Martian Plains, a warm breathing tone in the Martian Dunes, a deep stone drone in the Martian Canyons, cold wind over ice on the Martian Polar Cap, and the warm hum of charged air in the Vapors of the Breath. Now and then something short sounds nearby — pebbles, a whirl of wind, cracking ice or a rockfall on Mars; thunder, sizzling acid and clinking glass on the Breath — and caves have their own rumble. Every few seconds a quiet phrase of two to four notes plays on different steps of a scale (a mallet on Mars, a chime on the Breath).

The ambience sits under the game's own sounds: footsteps, mobs and your voice chat stay on top. Its volume is the game's "Ambient/Environment" slider; under a roof and in caves the background is quieter, and so it is during world weather (weather has its own sounds). On low Mod graphics the notes play less often and the phrases are shorter. The mod setting Ambience sounds turns it off.

Screen layout

The mod’s hints, alarms and messages never cover each other: every line has its own place, and when it gets crowded the less important one gives way.

  • Top center — boss bars, their phase hints and event banners, then the jump progress, the autopilot, anomaly alarms and the mod line while in a ship.
  • Below the crosshair — central alarms (“no fuel for take-off”, a comet jet) and the ore scanner summary; the more important one stays closer to the crosshair.
  • Above the hotbar — the mod line; it steps around recent chat messages and the suit bars, and fades over the chat while you are typing.
  • Corners — the comms header (ship, deck, big moments) top left, the target and effect icons top right, coordinates bottom right. The task card yields its corner to the comms header.

When there is not enough room, a less important line first gets shorter (a single line) and then folds into “+N” at the end of its column — and comes back as soon as there is room again.

The mod's windows follow the game's GUI Scale setting, like the game's own screens: Auto looks as before, and a smaller scale makes a window smaller and gives it more room (on 1366×768 and larger screens); to make them larger at the same scale, use Mod window size. The mod's HUD stays as it is.
